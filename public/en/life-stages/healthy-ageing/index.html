Healthy ageing

Checked: 2026-11-25Evidence: High for a balanced diet, regular activity and no tobacco lowering non-communicable disease risk (WHO); High for exercise reducing falls (Cochrane 2019); loneliness and early death is observational data (WHO 2025)Ages: 40-64, 65+Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

Educational content; it does not replace regular check-ups. Detailed food and activity advice for older adults: see Older adults (65+).

Key points

  • WHO describes healthy ageing as keeping the ability to do what you value, with surroundings that support you as health changes. It is not the same as "having no disease". Verified
  • Three habits WHO names: a balanced diet, regular physical activity, not using tobacco. They lower the risk of non-communicable diseases and delay dependence on care. Verified
  • Watch early for signs of decline: memory, walking, eating and weight loss, vision, hearing, mood, incontinence, falls. These are the problems WHO's ICOPE approach to older people's care looks for. Verified

What the standards say

Standard Main points
WHO – Ageing and health (1 Oct 2025) By 2030, about 1 in 6 people worldwide will be 60 or older. Common conditions: hearing loss, cataracts, refractive errors, back and neck pain, osteoarthritis, COPD, diabetes, depression, dementia; many people have several at once. Geriatric syndromes: frailty, incontinence, falls, delirium, pressure ulcers. Ageism is a barrier
WHO – ICOPE (integrated care for older people; handbook 2nd ed., 22 Sep 2025) Focus on keeping "intrinsic capacity" (physical and mental capacities). Detect early: cognitive decline, limited mobility, hearing loss, malnutrition, vision impairment, depressive symptoms, incontinence, falls
WHO – Commission on Social Connection (30 Jun 2025) About 1 in 6 people worldwide are lonely. Loneliness is linked with more than 871,000 deaths a year (estimate for 2014–2019) and with higher risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes, depression and anxiety
Vietnam – Law on the Elderly 39/2009/QH12 (in force 1 Jul 2010), Article 2 Older people are Vietnamese citizens aged 60 or over
Vietnam – Decision 1116/QĐ-TTg (22 Jun 2026) amending Decision 1579/QĐ-TTg Target from 2026: older people get a free periodic health check or screening at least once a year and a health record
Australia – Healthdirect (70 and over) Every year: blood pressure, falls risk assessment, dental check, hearing test, eye test
UK – NHS From 60, free eye tests every 2 years; from 70, every year

How to do it – the minimal version

  1. Have a health check every year. In Vietnam, ask your commune or ward health station about the free check-up for older people under Decision 1116. [Verified for the policy target; local implementation: not verified]
  2. Get eyes and ears checked regularly. Turning the TV up or struggling to follow conversation: get a hearing test. NHS: poor eyesight and ear problems can affect balance. [Verified – Healthdirect, NHS]
  3. Move every day, including balance and strength work. See Strength and balance. Verified
  4. Eat enough and watch your weight. Unplanned weight loss is a reason to see a doctor. See Older adults (65+). [Verified for malnutrition as an ICOPE issue; unplanned weight loss: Expert judgement]
  5. Stay connected: see friends and relatives, join an older people's club or community activities. [Verified for the role of social connection – WHO; how: Inference]
  6. Don't smoke; limit alcohol. See Alcohol. [Verified for tobacco – WHO]

Common misunderstandings

  • "Old age means weakness, confusion and falls." WHO treats these as syndromes to detect and care for, not as inevitable. Exercise reduces falls (Cochrane 2019). Verified
  • "Hearing loss is just something to live with." Hearing loss is one of the problems WHO advises detecting early. Verified
  • "Loneliness is only a feeling." WHO: loneliness is linked with early death and many long-term conditions. This is observational data and does not prove cause. Verified

Who should take care

  • People who have fallen, feel dizzy, or have osteoporosis or heart disease: ask a health worker before exercising harder. Expert judgement
  • People taking many medicines: ask a doctor to review the list regularly. Expert judgement
  • The site gives no advice on "anti-ageing" supplements. Expert judgement

When to see a health worker

  • Clear memory loss, confusion about days and times, getting lost; long-lasting low mood; unexplained weight loss. Expert judgement
  • A fall, even without injury; walking getting harder; worse vision or hearing. Expert judgement
  • Call 113 straight away for sudden confusion, weakness on one side, a drooping face or trouble speaking. Expert judgement
  • Older people facing abuse or neglect, or with thoughts of self-harm: see Find help.

Level of evidence

  • Balanced diet, activity and no tobacco lowering non-communicable disease risk: High (WHO). Verified
  • Exercise reducing falls in older people: High (Cochrane 2019, see the Older adults page). Verified
  • Loneliness and early death: observational data, an association (WHO 2025). Verified
