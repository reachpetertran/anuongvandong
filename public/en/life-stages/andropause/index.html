The 'male menopause'

Checked: 2026-11-25Evidence: High for the slow age-related fall in testosterone (guideline consensus); Moderate for how testosterone deficiency is diagnosed (Endocrine Society 2018); benefits and risks of testosterone for older men without true deficiency: not established (FDA)Ages: 40-64, 65+Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

Educational content, not a diagnosis. The site gives no guidance on testosterone or "testosterone-boosting" supplements.

Key points

  • "Male menopause" (andropause) is a misleading term. Testosterone in men falls slowly, about 1% a year from around 30–40, not suddenly like oestrogen at menopause. This fall on its own rarely causes problems. Verified
  • Midlife symptoms (tiredness, low sex drive, erection problems, irritability, poor sleep) are often linked to lifestyle, stress, depression or long-term conditions. Verified
  • Late-onset hypogonadism is real but uncommon. It is diagnosed only with symptoms and clearly low fasting morning blood tests, repeated at least twice. Verified

What the standards say

Standard Main recommendations
UK – NHS (reviewed 13 Oct 2022) "Male menopause" is misleading. Testosterone falls about 1% a year from around 30–40. Common causes: poor sleep, diet, lack of exercise, heavy drinking, smoking, stress, depression. Late-onset hypogonadism is uncommon and more likely with obesity or type 2 diabetes. Testosterone only if a specialist confirms a deficiency
Australia – Healthdirect (reviewed Sep 2022) Misleading term. Healthy older men may have lower testosterone due to ageing; this is generally not a medical concern
US – Endocrine Society (2018) No routine screening of all men. Diagnose only with symptoms and clearly, consistently low testosterone; measure fasting in the morning and repeat to confirm. Recommends against routinely prescribing testosterone to all men over 65 with low levels
US – FDA (updated 28 Feb 2025) Testosterone is approved only for men with low testosterone plus an associated medical condition. Benefit and safety are not established for low testosterone due to ageing. Since 2025: an added raised blood pressure warning for all testosterone products
Vietnam No Ministry of Health guidance verified
WHO No specific recommendation verified

How to do it – the minimal version

  1. Check five things first: sleep, activity, alcohol, smoking, stress. The NHS lists these as common causes. See Active minutes, Alcohol, Stress. Verified
  2. Do strength training twice a week to keep muscle with age. See Strength and balance. [Verified – general activity guidance]
  3. See a doctor if symptoms persist. They will ask about work and family life to look for psychological causes first. [Verified – NHS]
  4. If a hormone problem is suspected: fasting morning test, repeated to confirm. One low result is not enough. [Verified – Endocrine Society]
  5. Don't buy testosterone yourself or products sold as "testosterone boosters". Expert judgement

Common misunderstandings

  • "Men go through menopause too." NHS, Healthdirect: there is no sudden hormone drop in men. Verified
  • "Tired with low libido at 50 means low testosterone; topping up fixes it." The Endocrine Society advises against testing and treating healthy men; FDA: benefits and risks are not established for age-related low testosterone. Verified
  • "Testosterone is a harmless tonic." The FDA requires a raised blood pressure warning. The Endocrine Society advises against starting it in men planning children soon, with breast or prostate cancer, or with some heart and blood vessel conditions. Verified

Who should take care

  • Men with obesity or type 2 diabetes: the NHS says late-onset hypogonadism is more likely; get checked if you have symptoms. Verified
  • Men planning to have children soon: the Endocrine Society advises against starting testosterone. Verified
  • Bodybuilders using anabolic steroids: there are health risks; ask a doctor. Expert judgement

When to see a health worker

  • Low sex drive, erection problems, tiredness or low mood lasting weeks. [Verified – NHS]
  • Breast enlargement, loss of body hair, shrinking testicles: see a doctor to look for a hormone cause. Expert judgement
  • Long-lasting low mood or thoughts of harming yourself: get support now. See Find help. Expert judgement

Level of evidence

  • Testosterone falls slowly with age, unlike menopause: High (NHS, Healthdirect, Endocrine Society agree). Verified
  • Diagnosis (symptoms + repeated morning test): Moderate by the Endocrine Society 2018 grading. Verified
  • Testosterone for older men with age-related low testosterone: benefits and risks not established (FDA 2025). Verified
