Puberty

Checked: 2026-11-25Evidence: High for the age range of puberty and the early/late thresholds (guideline consensus); parenting support is practical advice (Healthdirect), not gradedAges: 6-11, 12-17Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

Written for parents and for teenagers, at the level of school health materials. It does not replace a check-up with a children's doctor.

Key points

  • Puberty usually starts at 8–13 in girls and 9–14 in boys (Vietnam's Ministry of Health; NHS: 8–14). Girls usually start 1–2 years before boys. Every child develops at their own pace. Verified
  • Ministry of Health (Vietnam): puberty before 8 in girls, before 9 in boys is early. NHS: not started by 15 may mean delayed puberty. Both are worth a check-up. Verified
  • Puberty brings changes in mood, sleep and energy. WHO: about 1 in 7 young people aged 10–19 have a mental health condition, mostly unrecognised. Verified

What the standards say

Ages Verified

Standard Recommendation
UK – NHS (reviewed 2 Apr 2026) Starts between 8 and 14. Before 8: may be early puberty. Not started by 15: may be delayed puberty. Main changes: hair in the armpits and genital area; boys: larger testicles and penis, deeper voice; girls: breast development, periods start
Australia – Healthdirect (reviewed Aug 2025) Girls: around 8–13. Boys: often around 11–12. Mood, energy and sleep patterns often shift
US – AAP (HealthyChildren) Girls usually start 1–2 years before boys; it can be normal to start from 8 (girls) or 9 (boys). Many girls have their first period around 12, on average 2–3 years after breast development begins
WHO Adolescence is 10–19 years. No specific recommendation on puberty ages verified
Vietnam – Ministry of Health (Decision 3261/QĐ-BYT, 4 Nov 2024) Girls usually start between 8 and 13, boys between 9 and 14. Puberty before 8 in girls and before 9 in boys counts as early. Children today tend to start earlier than before. In Vietnam, adolescents are aged 10–18

Adolescent mental health Verified

Standard Content
WHO (1 Sep 2025) About 1 in 7 (14.3%) of 10–19-year-olds have a mental health condition, mostly unrecognised and untreated. Physical, emotional and social changes, plus poverty or violence, make young people more vulnerable
WHO Half of adult mental health conditions start by age 18
Australia – Healthdirect Seek support if low mood lasts more than 2 weeks, if there are big changes in thoughts, feelings or behaviour, or if it affects school, home or friendships

How to do it – the minimal version

  1. Talk before puberty starts, so your child knows what's coming and that it's normal. [Verified – AAP]
  2. Listen without interrupting, ask with curiosity; give them space and don't fix everything for them. [Verified – Healthdirect]
  3. Keep sleep regular. Teen body clocks shift later. See Teenagers 12–17 for sleep hours. [Verified – Healthdirect]
  4. Model healthy eating, encourage activities in and out of school. [Verified – Healthdirect]
  5. Keep clear boundaries and give safe chances for independence. When anger flares: keep everyone safe, wait for calm, consistent non-violent consequences. [Verified – Healthdirect]
  6. Hygiene: regular washing; for girls, prepare ahead for the first period. Expert judgement

Common misunderstandings

  • "Starting later than friends means something is wrong." Each child has their own pace; the NHS uses age 15 as the point where puberty may be delayed. Verified
  • "Moody teenagers are normal, leave them alone." Healthdirect: low mood lasting more than 2 weeks or big changes are a reason to seek support. Verified
  • "Talking about puberty early makes children curious." The AAP advises helping children anticipate changes and reassuring them that they are normal. Verified

Who should take care

  • Children with long-term conditions, on long-term medicines, underweight or in intense sport: puberty may come later; follow up with a doctor. Expert judgement
  • Children very worried about their body or dieting strictly: ask a health worker. The site gives no weight-loss advice for children. Expert judgement

When to see a health worker

  • Signs of puberty before 8 (girls) or before 9 (boys); no signs by 15. [Verified – Ministry of Health, NHS]
  • Low mood for more than 2 weeks; big changes in thinking or behaviour; anger that continues despite your efforts. [Verified – Healthdirect]
  • A child with thoughts of self-harm, or facing abuse or violence: get help now, call 111 (Vietnam's national child protection hotline). See Find help.

Level of evidence

  • Age range of puberty and early/late thresholds: High (Vietnam's Ministry of Health, NHS, Healthdirect, AAP agree). Verified
  • How parents can help: practical advice from Healthdirect and the AAP; not graded by the site. Verified
