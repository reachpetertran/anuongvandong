Teenagers (12–17 years)

Checked: 2026-11-25Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

Key points

  • Teenagers should do an average of at least 60 minutes a day of moderate to vigorous activity, with at least 3 days a week of vigorous and muscle- and bone-strengthening activity (Vietnam, WHO, US). Verified
  • Sleep: AASM advises 8–10 hours for ages 13–18; Australia advises 8–10 hours for ages 14–17. A 12-year-old falls in AASM's 6–12 group (9–12 hours). Verified
  • In Vietnam, the law bans people under 18 from drinking alcohol. The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) advises children and adolescents not to use energy drinks. Verified

What the standards say

Activity and screens Verified

Standard Activity Screens, sitting
Vietnam – Department of Disease Prevention (Decision 103/QĐ-PB, 13 Jul 2026), 5–17 An average of at least 60 minutes a day on all 7 days, moderate and vigorous; vigorous muscle-strengthening at least 3 times a week; bone-strengthening at least 3 times a week Limit sedentary time
WHO (2020), 5–17 An average of at least 60 minutes a day across the week; vigorous and muscle- and bone-strengthening at least 3 days a week Limit sitting, particularly recreational screen time
US – PAG/CDC, 6–17 60 minutes or more every day; at least 3 days a week of vigorous, muscle- and bone-strengthening activity Not verified
Australia – 24-Hour Guidelines (2019), 5–17 At least 60 minutes moderate to vigorous every day Recreational screens no more than 2 hours a day (schoolwork excluded)
UK – CMOs (updated 2026), 5–18 An average of at least 60 minutes a day across the week Reduce sitting and lying; break up with activity

Sleep Verified

Standard Recommendation
US – AASM (2016, endorsed by AAP) Ages 13–18: 8–10 hours per 24 hours (age 12: 9–12 hours)
Australia – 24-Hour Guidelines Ages 14–17: 8–10 hours of uninterrupted sleep a night (12–13: 9–11 hours)
WHO, Vietnam No sleep-hours recommendation verified

How to do it – minimal version

  1. Pick activities you enjoy so you keep them up: football, badminton, basketball, swimming, skipping, group dance; walking or cycling to school. [Verified for examples – Decision 103]
  2. During long study sessions, get up and move between sessions. [Verified – limit sedentary time]
  3. Switch screens off 1 hour before sleep and keep phones out of the bedroom. Keep regular bed and wake times, weekends included. [Verified – Australia]
  4. Water instead of milk tea and soft drinks; no energy drinks. See Caffeine, Free sugars. Verified
  5. No alcohol (illegal under 18 in Vietnam). See Alcohol. Verified
  6. Eat full meals, with dairy and protein: the pyramids for this age call for 6 units of dairy a day. Verified

Common misconceptions

  • "Teens stay up late, 6 hours of sleep is enough." AASM and Australia advise 8–10 hours at this age. Verified
  • "Energy drinks help you stay alert to study." AAP advises children and adolescents not to use energy drinks. Verified
  • "Skip meals to get thin." The pyramids for this age call for enough from every group. The site gives no weight-loss advice for under-18s; if you are worried about weight, see a health worker. Expert judgement

Who should take care

  • Teens with heart disease, asthma, epilepsy, a disability or under treatment: ask a doctor about suitable activity. Expert judgement
  • Intermittent fasting is not for teenagers (Healthdirect, Australia). See Intermittent fasting. Verified
  • If you worry a lot about weight, body shape or eating: talk to a parent, teacher or health worker. Expert judgement
  • If you are very tired, short of breath, dizzy or have chest pain during activity: stop and rest (Decision 103). Verified

When to see a health worker

  • Lasting sadness or loss of interest, weeks of poor sleep, overwhelming study stress. See Stress. Expert judgement
  • Chest pain or fainting during activity. Expert judgement
  • Bullying, abuse or violence: call 111 (Vietnam's national child protection hotline). Thoughts of harming yourself: get help now – see Find help.
