Older adults (65 and over)

Checked: 2026-11-25Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

Key points

  • The standards have separate advice for older adults: Vietnam starts at 60 for activity advice; WHO, Australia, the US and the UK start at 65. Vietnam's National Institute of Nutrition has a separate food pyramid for older adults. Verified
  • The biggest difference from younger adults: add balance and strength activity to prevent falls; WHO advises at least 3 days a week. Verified
  • Australia and the CDC (US) advise older adults to sleep 7–8 hours. Australia stresses regular bed and wake-up times. Verified

Movement: what the standards say

Vietnam Verified

Source Recommendation
Department of Disease Prevention (Decision 103/QĐ-PB, 13 Jul 2026) From 60: at least 3 days a week of multicomponent activity, e.g. dancing, standing on one leg, walking backwards, walking sideways
National Institute of Nutrition – older adults pyramid (2025) Physical activity at least 30 minutes a day

Other standards Verified

Standard Recommendation
WHO (2020) As for adults: 150–300 minutes moderate or 75–150 minutes vigorous a week; muscle strengthening at least 2 days. Plus: varied multicomponent activity emphasising functional balance and strength, at moderate or greater intensity, at least 3 days a week, to improve function and prevent falls
US – CDC Each week: at least 150 minutes moderate (e.g. 30 minutes × 5 days) or 75 minutes vigorous; muscle strengthening 2 days; plus balance activities
Australia – 24-Hour Movement Guidelines (2026) Muscle strengthening at least 2 days a week; functional, balance and coordination activity at least 3 days a week (as for 18–64); 7–8 hours of sleep with regular bed and wake-up times
UK – NHS Be active every day, even if light; strength, balance and flexibility activities at least 2 days a week; 150 minutes moderate or 75 minutes vigorous a week. Speak to a GP first if you haven't exercised for a while or have a health condition

The standards agree: move every day, with strength and balance training. They differ in balance days: WHO, Vietnam and Australia say 3; the UK says 2. Verified

What is specific to older adults

Preventing falls. Cochrane review 2019 (108 RCTs, average age 76): exercise reduced the rate of falls by 23%; balance and functional exercise by 24%. High-certainty evidence. Verified See Strength and balance.

Keeping muscle. According to Healthdirect (Australia), muscle mass declines faster after 50; strength training is the main measure, with enough protein. Verified Australia sets higher protein needs for people over 70 – see Protein.

Drinking water. Thirst can weaken with age; drink at regular times. See Drinking water. Expert judgement

Sleep. Australia and the CDC: 7–8 hours. See How much sleep, Sleep habits. Verified

Screening. Many programmes run to 74–75 (e.g. bowel: UK 50–74, Australia 45–74, US 45–75). Beyond that, whether to continue is a decision for the doctor based on each person's health. See the tables on the Middle age 40–64 page. Verified

Intermittent fasting is not for older adults (Healthdirect, Australia). See Intermittent fasting. Verified

Who should take care

  • People who have fallen, often feel dizzy, have osteoporosis, heart disease or uncontrolled high blood pressure, or are 75+ and starting exercise: ask a health worker for suitable exercises. The site has not published exercise programmes for these groups. Expert judgement
  • People losing weight, with weak muscles or poor appetite: don't diet or fast on your own; ask a health worker. Expert judgement
  • People on several medicines: ask a doctor before a big diet change, potassium-based salt, or supplements. Expert judgement

When to see a health worker

  • A fall, even without injury; worsening balance. Expert judgement
  • Unexplained weight loss, lasting poor appetite, weakness. Expert judgement
  • New confusion, passing little urine, dry mouth (possible dehydration). Expert judgement
  • Chest pain, difficulty breathing, a drooping face, weakness on one side: call emergency services (see Find help).
