Preschool (3–5 years)

Checked: 2026-11-25Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

Key points

  • Children aged 3–5 should be active for at least 180 minutes a day, including at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity. Vietnam and WHO agree on this figure. Verified
  • Recreational screen time no more than 1 hour a day, less is better; 10–13 hours of sleep in 24 hours, including naps. Verified
  • Vietnam's National Institute of Nutrition has a separate food pyramid for 3–5-year-olds, with less salt and sugar than for adults. Verified

Age bands: Vietnam (Decision 103) and WHO use "3 to under 5"; from 5, children follow the 5–17 advice. Australia, the US and AASM use 3–5. Verified

The pyramid also says: physical activity at least 60 minutes a day. The site built this table from the figures and does not reproduce the pyramid image. How to read the pyramid: see The plate and the food pyramid. Verified

How to do it – minimal version

  1. Let children play actively throughout the day: running, jumping, safe climbing, throwing and catching, tricycles. Add up to 3 hours. [Verified for the example activities – Decision 103]
  2. Don't keep children sitting still for more than 1 hour at a time in prams, high chairs or car seats. [Verified – Australia]
  3. Recreational screens under 1 hour a day. Replace with reading and storytelling together. Verified
  4. Keep sleep times regular, 10–13 hours including naps. [Verified for the hours]
  5. Season children's food more lightly than adults': the 3–5 pyramid sets salt under 3 g and sugar under 15 g a day (under 3 units × 5 g). Verified
  6. Water instead of sugary drinks. See Free sugars. Verified

Common misconceptions

  • "Lots of running around tires them out, so let them rest in front of the TV." The standards say more activity is better at this age, and limit recreational screens. Verified
  • "They can just eat what adults eat." The 3–5 pyramid sets lower salt and sugar than for adults (under 3 versus under 5 units). Verified

Who should take care

  • Children with heart disease, asthma, epilepsy, a physical disability or under treatment: ask a doctor about suitable activity. Expert judgement
  • Children with food allergies, underweight or overweight, or long-term poor appetite: diet needs individual advice from a health worker. The site gives no weight-loss advice for children. Expert judgement
  • Children under 3: see Young infants, Starting solids, Toddlers 1–2.

When to see a health worker

  • Delayed speech or movement compared with peers; loud snoring or pauses in breathing during sleep. Expert judgement
  • Poor appetite, weight loss or no weight gain for several months. Expert judgement
  • Difficulty breathing, turning blue or fainting during play: call emergency services (see Find help). Abuse or violence against a child: call 111 (Vietnam's national child protection hotline).
