Habits that help sleep

Checked: 2026-11-25Evidence: Moderate for individual habits; Low for learning habits alone as a treatment for insomniaAges: 18-39, 40-64Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original. Quotes from Vietnamese documents are unofficial translations.

Key points

  • Australia (2026) advises 7–9 hours of sleep with consistent bed and wake-up times. The NHS (UK) advises going to bed and waking up at the same time every day. Verified
  • Each habit (coffee, alcohol, timing, activity…) has evidence linking it to sleep. But learning sleep habits alone has limited effect once someone has real insomnia. Verified
  • Chronic insomnia needs a health worker. Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is the first treatment according to the American College of Physicians (ACP). Verified

What the standards say

Australia – 24-Hour Guidelines (2026) 7–9 hours of good-quality sleep with consistent bed and wake times. The glossary lists sleep-hygiene habits: minimise caffeine, limit alcohol, minimise screen light after sunset, use naps to get enough sleep but avoid napping too long, a dark, quiet, comfortable bedroom. Verified
UK – NHS For insomnia: go to bed and wake up at the same time; relax before bed; do not use devices right before bed; do not nap during the day; do not sleep in after a bad night; do not drive when sleepy. Verified

Note: Australia accepts short naps; the NHS advises people with insomnia not to nap. The two pieces of advice target different groups. Inference

How to do it – the minimal version

1. Fix your wake-up time, weekends included. A leaflet from an NHS hospital says a fixed wake-up time is probably more important than a fixed bedtime. Go to bed only when sleepy. Verified

2. Stop coffee early. Gardiner 2023 review (24 studies): a cup of coffee with about 107 mg of caffeine should be drunk at least 8.8 hours before bedtime to avoid losing sleep time. Verified

Hypothetical example: if you sleep at 10 pm, have your last coffee before about 1 pm. Strong Vietnamese drip coffee may contain more caffeine than the 107 mg in the study. Hypothetical example

3. Avoid alcohol in the 4 hours before bed. This advice comes from an NHS hospital leaflet. Verified

4. Put the phone away before bed. NHS: do not watch TV or use your phone right before bed, because the light makes you more alert. Verified

5. Keep the bedroom dark, quiet and comfortable. Verified Use curtains or earplugs if needed. Inference

6. Write your worries down. Before bed, list tomorrow's tasks to clear your mind. The NHS suggests this. Verified

7. If you have been lying awake for more than 30 minutes, get up. Do something relaxing, then go back to bed when sleepy. Verified

8. Be active during the day. NHS: regular activity helps you sleep better. You can add Five minutes of slow breathing before bed. Verified

Naps: if you sleep well at night, a short nap is fine. If you struggle to sleep at night, try dropping naps for a few weeks. Inference

Common misunderstandings

  • "Exercising in the evening causes insomnia." Irish 2015 review: current evidence does not support this claim. If you find it hard to sleep after late exercise, exercise earlier. Verified
  • "A drink helps me sleep." The NHS says alcohol can disrupt sleep. Verified
  • "If I can't sleep, I should stay in bed until I do." An NHS leaflet advises not lying in bed worrying for more than 30 minutes; get up and do something relaxing. Verified
  • "After a bad night, I'll sleep in." The NHS advises sticking to your regular sleeping hours instead. Verified
  • "If I follow all the habits, my insomnia will go." Sleep-habit education alone has limited effect on real insomnia. Chronic insomnia needs treatment. Verified

Who should take care

  • Night and shift workers: fixed times are hard; ask your workplace health service. Inference
  • Do not self-treat with sleeping pills or sleep aids for long periods. The ACP notes that sleep medicines can have serious side effects. Verified
  • Drivers and machine operators: do not drive when sleepy. Verified
  • This site does not teach self-directed "sleep restriction". It is part of CBT-I and needs a trained professional. Expert judgement
  • Pregnant women and people with a chronic condition: ask a health worker before making big changes to your sleep schedule. Inference

When to see a health worker

  • Trouble sleeping on many nights that goes on; the NHS calls insomnia lasting 3 months or longer long-term insomnia. If changing habits has not helped, get checked. See Signs of chronic insomnia. Verified
  • Heavy daytime sleepiness affecting work or driving. Expert judgement
  • Loud snoring, or someone notices you stop breathing during sleep. Expert judgement
  • Poor sleep together with lasting low mood or anxiety. Expert judgement

Level of evidence

  • Individual habits: Moderate. Irish 2015 review: epidemiological and experimental research generally supports links between individual habits and night-time sleep. Verified
  • Caffeine: Moderate. Gardiner 2023: caffeine cut total sleep time by 45 minutes and sleep efficiency by 7%, and added 9 minutes to the time taken to fall asleep. Verified
  • Learning sleep habits alone to treat insomnia: Low. Evidence is limited and inconclusive. Verified
  • CBT-I for chronic insomnia: High. The ACP grades it a strong recommendation with moderate-quality evidence. Verified
