Signs of chronic insomnia

Checked: 2026-11-25Evidence: High for CBT-I as first-line treatment (AASM 2021 strong recommendation; ACP 2016; NICE CKS); Low for sleep-habit education alone to treat chronic insomniaAges: 18-39, 40-64, 65+Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

This article helps you recognise signs worth a check-up. It is not for self-diagnosis. The site gives no guidance on sleeping pills.

Key points

  • The diagnostic standards (ICSD-3, DSM-5) call it chronic insomnia when: trouble falling asleep, waking often or waking too early happens at least 3 nights a week for at least 3 months, despite enough time and a suitable place to sleep, and affects the daytime. Verified
  • First-line treatment is cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I). The US, UK and Australia agree. Sleeping pills are for short-term use only, when prescribed. Verified
  • Loud snoring, stopping breathing in sleep and excessive daytime sleepiness may be sleep apnoea – a different condition that needs its own check-up. Verified

What the standards say

What counts as chronic insomnia Verified

Standard Definition
ICSD-3 (AASM) and DSM-5 Trouble starting or staying asleep, at least 3 nights a week, for at least 3 months, despite enough opportunity to sleep; with at least one daytime effect: fatigue, poor concentration or memory, irritability, sleepiness, reduced performance at study or work. Under 3 months: short-term insomnia
UK – NHS (reviewed 19 Mar 2024) Insomnia lasting 3 months or longer is long-term; under 3 months is short-term
Vietnam No original Ministry of Health document on diagnosing or treating insomnia verified

Treatment Verified

Standard Recommendation
US – AASM (2021) Strong recommendation: multicomponent CBT-I for adults with chronic insomnia. Sleep hygiene should not be used alone as the treatment. CBT-I usually takes 4–8 sessions
US – ACP (2016) All adults with chronic insomnia should get CBT-I as the first treatment
UK – NICE CKS, NHS CBT-I is first-line for adults of any age, face to face or through a digital programme. Sleeping pills are now rarely prescribed; if used, for a few days, a few weeks at most
Australia – Healthdirect, Sleep Health Foundation CBT-I is first-line. Sleeping pills are less effective than CBT-I and do not cure insomnia because they don't address the cause

CBT-I includes: learning about sleep, stimulus control (only go to bed when sleepy), limiting time in bed, relaxation, and changing worried thoughts about sleep. It can be done face to face or through an online programme. [Verified – AASM, Sleep Health Foundation]

How to do it – the minimal version

  1. Keep a sleep diary for 2 weeks: time to bed, estimated time asleep, number of wakings, time up, daytime tiredness. Take it to your appointment. Expert judgement
  2. Check 3 questions: at least 3 nights a week? For 3 months or more? Days affected? If all 3 are "yes", see a health worker. [Verified for the criteria]
  3. Use basic sleep habits while you wait. See Habits that help sleep. Verified
  4. At the appointment, ask about CBT-I. Mention medicines, alcohol, coffee and shift work. [Verified – the NHS lists these causes]
  5. Ask a bed partner to notice whether you snore loudly, stop breathing or gasp in sleep. [Verified – NHS]

Common misunderstandings

  • "Just take a sleeping pill." NHS: sleeping pills are now rarely prescribed and only short-term. Australia: pills don't treat the cause. Verified
  • "Few hours of sleep means insomnia." By definition, insomnia needs a daytime effect and happens despite enough opportunity to sleep. Sleeping little because you work late is sleep deprivation, not insomnia. [Verified for the definition; the distinction: Inference]
  • "Good habits alone will fix it." AASM: sleep hygiene should not be the only treatment for chronic insomnia. Verified

Who should take care

  • The site does not teach do-it-yourself "sleep restriction". It can cause daytime sleepiness and needs a trained professional, especially for people who drive, operate machinery, have epilepsy or bipolar disorder. Expert judgement
  • Long-term sleeping-pill users: don't stop suddenly; talk to a doctor. Expert judgement
  • Insomnia from pain, night-time urination, breathlessness, depression, anxiety or menopause: the cause needs treatment too. See Menopause, Stress. [Verified – the NHS lists these causes]

When to see a health worker

  • Changing sleep habits hasn't helped; you've had trouble sleeping for months; insomnia makes daily life hard to cope with. [Verified – NHS]
  • Loud snoring, stopping breathing or gasping in sleep, always very tired in the day: get checked for sleep apnoea. Don't drive when sleepy. [Verified – NHS]
  • Insomnia with long-lasting low mood or thoughts of harming yourself: get support now. See Find help. Expert judgement

Level of evidence

  • CBT-I for chronic insomnia in adults: High. AASM 2021 strong recommendation (GRADE); ACP 2016 strong recommendation. Verified
  • Sleep-habit education alone as the only treatment: Low. AASM advises against using it alone. Verified
