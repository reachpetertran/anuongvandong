What this site is

  • A free self-study site on eating, movement, sleep, breathing and meditation for each age group.
  • Every article is based on official guidelines (WHO, Vietnam's Ministry of Health, US, Australian and UK health agencies) and peer-reviewed research.
  • Where standards disagree, the site puts them side by side and explains the context. It does not take sides.
  • Author: Peter Tran. Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional.

What this site is not

  • No diagnosis, no prescriptions, no personal advice.
  • Nothing for sale: no supplements, medicines, formula or courses. No ads.
  • It does not collect your health information. No cookies, no log-in.

If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or care for a child under 2: ask a health worker before applying anything here.

"Associated with" is not "causes"

Many nutrition studies are observational: they watch how people live and compare. They show that two things go together; they do not prove that one causes the other. The three examples below are illustrations, not real data. Hypothetical example

  1. Ice cream and drowning. Months with high ice-cream sales also have more drownings. Ice cream does not cause drowning. Both rise in hot weather.
  2. Walking and living longer. People who walk more may live longer. But people who are already ill tend to walk less. Poor health may cause less walking, not only the other way round.
  3. Breakfast and weight. Regular breakfast eaters may weigh less. But they may also sleep enough, smoke less and drink less. Such factors are called confounders.

So on this site, observational results are written as "associated with", never as "cuts X%" as if certain. Showing cause needs randomised controlled trials (RCTs) or a pooled analysis of many RCTs (meta-analysis).

When to see a health worker

  • You have new symptoms, or symptoms that last or get worse.
  • You have a long-term condition and want to change how you eat or exercise.
  • Danger signs: call emergency services now. See Find help.
