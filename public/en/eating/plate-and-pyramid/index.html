The plate and the food pyramid

Checked: 2026-11-25Evidence: Not applicable – summary of official guidance (National Institute of Nutrition, WHO, US, Australia, UK)Ages: 18-39, 40-64Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.

Key points

  • Vietnam's National Institute of Nutrition issued a set of 7 food pyramids by age group (Decision 640/QĐ-VDD, 26 May 2025). The adult pyramid gives average intake for 1 person per day, counted in "food units". Verified
  • Base (eat enough): grains and tubers 14–17 units; vegetables 3–4; fruit 3; protein foods 5–6; dairy 3–4; water 8–12 cups. Top (limit): fats and oils 5–6, sugar under 5, salt under 5 units. Verified
  • All standards share the same idea: plenty of vegetables and fruit, whole grains first, enough protein, little salt, sugar and ultra-processed food. They differ in the picture and in how portions are counted. Verified

What the standards say

Vietnam – Food pyramid for adults (National Institute of Nutrition, Decision 640/QĐ-VDD, 26 May 2025) Verified

Grains, tubers and products (eat enough) 14–17 units 20 g carbohydrate ½ small bowl of white rice (50 g); ½ small bowl of phở noodles (62 g); ½ baguette (38 g)
Leafy and root vegetables (eat enough) 3–4 units 80 g raw clean vegetables ½ small bowl of boiled greens (70 g); 1 tomato (80 g)
Ripe fruit (eat enough) 3 units 80 g 1 peeled banana; 1 pomelo segment
Meat, seafood, eggs, protein-rich seeds (eat enough) 5–6 units 7 g protein 3 slices boiled lean pork (27 g); 1 hen's egg (47 g); 2 pieces fried tofu (52 g)
Milk and dairy (eat enough) 3–4 units 100 mg calcium 100 ml milk; 100 g yoghurt; 15 g cheese

Sugar, sweets (limit) under 5 units 5 g sugar

Other standards Verified

Standard Main recommendations
WHO (Healthy diet fact sheet, 26 Jan 2026) Four principles: adequacy, balance, moderation, diversity. Carbohydrates mainly from whole grains, vegetables, fruit and pulses. At least 400 g of fruit and vegetables a day (not counting potatoes or cassava). Salt under 5 g a day.
US – DGA 2025–2030 (pp. 2–4) Brings back a pyramid in place of the MyPlate plate. For a 2,000 kcal pattern: vegetables 3 servings, fruit 2, whole grains 2–4, dairy 3 servings a day. This edition is being debated by nutrition experts (see Protein).
Australia – Australian Guide to Healthy Eating (NHMRC 2013) Five food groups. Ages 19–50: vegetables and legumes 5–6 serves, fruit 2, grains 6, protein foods 2.5–3, dairy 2.5 serves a day (women 51–70: grains 4, dairy 4).
UK – Eatwell Guide (NHS) Fruit and vegetables just over ⅓ of what we eat each day, at least 5 portions; starchy foods (wholegrain where possible) just over ⅓; 6–8 drinks a day. The balance need not be exact at every meal – over a day or a week.

Why the portion numbers differ: each country defines "a serving" differently (Vietnam by grams of nutrient, Australia and the UK by amount of food). Don't compare the numbers directly across countries. Inference

How to do it – minimal version

  1. Look at your plate at main meals: are there vegetables, a protein dish, and is the rice far larger than the vegetables? Inference
  2. Vegetables at both main meals. 3–4 units of vegetables is roughly 1.5–2 small bowls of boiled greens a day. [Inference, converted from the pyramid's ½ bowl = 70 g example]
  3. Fruit instead of sweets. 3 units of fruit = 3 pieces of about 80 g. Verified
  4. Swap some white rice for brown rice, maize or sweet potato when you can. [Verified for WHO's whole-grain advice]
  5. The top of the pyramid is for limiting: salt under 5 g, sugar under 25 g (5 units × 5 g) a day. See Salt, Free sugars. Verified

Common misconceptions

  • "You must eat exactly the right units every day." The pyramid gives averages; the UK Eatwell Guide says balance over a day or week is enough. Verified
  • "Grains are at the base, so the more rice the better." The pyramid sets 14–17 units, together with vegetables, fruit, protein and dairy. It is an average; less active people may need less. Inference
  • "The new US pyramid is the latest, so it's the most correct." DGA 2025–2030 is being debated; the National Institute of Nutrition has published its own commentary. The site presents them side by side and does not take sides. Verified

Who should take care

  • Older adults, pregnant and breastfeeding women, children: use your own group's pyramid (same Decision 640/QĐ-VDD set). The site has tables for each group on the By age pages, for example Pregnancy and breastfeeding. Verified
  • People with diabetes, kidney, liver or heart disease, gout, or on a diet prescribed by a doctor: follow your doctor, not this general table. Expert judgement
  • People with a history of eating disorders: counting food units may not suit you; have professional support. Expert judgement

When to see a health worker

  • Unexplained weight loss or weight gain. Expert judgement
  • You want to make a big diet change while living with a long-term condition. Expert judgement
