Strength and balance

Checked: 2026-11-24Evidence: High for the strength recommendation (WHO: strong recommendation); Moderate for the 30–60 minutes a week figure (cohort studies)Ages: 18-39, 40-64Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original. Quotes from Vietnamese documents are unofficial translations.

Key points

  • Adults should do muscle-strengthening on at least 2 days a week, working all major muscle groups. Vietnam, WHO, the US, Australia and the UK all say this. Verified
  • Balance: Australia (2026) advises functional, balance and coordination activities on at least 3 days a week for adults aged 18–64 as well. Vietnam and WHO advise 3 days a week for older adults. Verified
  • About 30–60 minutes of muscle-strengthening a week is associated with a 10–20% lower risk of death. These are observational data. Verified

What the standards say

Standard Strength Balance / multicomponent
Vietnam – Department of Disease Prevention (Decision 103/QĐ-PB, 13 Jul 2026) Ages 18–59: at least 2 times a week, moderate or vigorous, all major muscle groups (section 2.3.2, p. 13) From 60: multicomponent activity on at least 3 days a week, e.g. dancing, standing on one leg, walking backwards or sideways (section 2.4.2, pp. 15–16)
WHO (2020) At least 2 days a week, moderate or greater intensity, all major muscle groups From 65: varied multicomponent activity emphasising functional balance and strength on at least 3 days a week
US – PAG, 2nd edition At least 2 days a week, all major muscle groups Older adults: multicomponent activity including balance training (no number of days given)
Australia – 24-Hour Guidelines (2026), 18–64 and 65+ 2 or more days, moderate or greater intensity, all main muscle groups 3 or more days a week of functional activities for mobility, balance and coordination – for adults aged 18–64 too
UK – CMOs (2019) Adults: at least 2 days a week Older adults: strength, balance and flexibility on at least 2 days a week

The standards agree on strength. They differ on balance: Australia includes it for all adults; the others list it only for older adults. Verified

What counts as "strength" and "balance"

  • Strength: activity that makes muscles work harder than usual. Lifting weights, resistance bands, or daily tasks such as carrying heavy loads all count. Verified
  • Major muscle groups: legs, hips, back, chest, abdomen, shoulders, arms. Verified
  • Balance: helps the body resist forces that cause a fall while standing still or moving. Examples: walking backwards, standing on one leg. Strengthening the back, abdomen and legs also improves balance. Verified
  • Enough effort: work until it is hard to do another repetition without help. This is how the CDC (US) describes it. Verified

How to do it – the minimal version

Step 1. Pick 2 days that are not back to back. For example Tuesday and Friday. 20–30 minutes per session is enough to start. Inference

Step 2. Warm up for 5 minutes. Roll the wrists and knees, stretch the shoulders, march gently on the spot. The Vietnamese handbook advises warming up before and cooling down after. Verified

Step 3. Do 5 exercises, 8–12 repetitions each, 1–3 rounds. Inference

Exercise Muscles
Sit-to-stand from a chair Legs, hips
Push-ups against a wall or table edge Chest, shoulders, arms
Glute bridge (lying on your back, lift the hips) Hips, lower back
Knee plank (forearms and knees, back straight) Abdomen, back
Carry two water bags; pull a resistance band Upper back, arms

Step 4. Add balance 3 times a week, 5 minutes each. Stand on one leg next to a table you can hold if needed. Walk heel-to-toe along a line. Take a few steps backwards and sideways. [Verified for the exercise names; the minutes are Inference]

Step 5. Progress slowly. Add repetitions and rounds first, then difficulty. The Vietnamese handbook notes that injury risk rises when the gap between your usual level and the new level is too large. Verified

Hypothetical example: on Tuesday and Friday, do the 5 exercises above, 2 rounds × 10 repetitions. On Monday, Wednesday and Saturday, stand on one leg while brushing your teeth, 30 seconds × 3 each side. Hypothetical example

Common misunderstandings

  • "Strength training is only for people who want big muscles." The standards advise it for every adult for health. Carrying loads and heavy gardening count. Verified
  • "The more the better." In Momma 2022 the association was J-shaped. There is no conclusive evidence that more than 1 hour a week further lowers the risk of death. Verified
  • "Balance is for old people." Australia's 2026 guidelines advise balance and coordination work on at least 3 days a week for adults aged 18–64 too. Verified
  • "If I walk a lot, I don't need strength work." All 5 standards list strength separately. Verified

Who should take care

  • People with a chronic condition (heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes…): Vietnam's Department of Disease Prevention advises considering a health check before starting. Verified
  • People with osteoporosis, joint pain, a history of falls, or aged 75 and over: exercise under the guidance of a health worker or physiotherapist. The site has no separate article for this group yet. Expert judgement
  • People with high blood pressure: do not hold your breath while straining; breathe out as you push or lift. Expert judgement
  • Pregnant and postpartum women: separate recommendations apply; this article does not. Inference
  • People who have been inactive for a long time: start light and build up. Verified

When to see a health worker

  • Stop if you feel very tired, dizzy, short of breath or a sharp pain, especially chest pain. Get checked if symptoms get worse. Verified
  • Joint or back pain that lasts after exercise, or a swollen joint. Expert judgement
  • You have fallen, or feel increasingly unsteady. Expert judgement
  • Severe chest pain or fainting: call emergency services (see Find help).

Level of evidence

  • Strength on 2 days a week: High. WHO 2020 grades it a strong recommendation. Verified
  • The 30–60 minutes a week figure: Moderate. A systematic review of cohort studies (Momma 2022): strength activity was associated with a 10–17% lower risk of death, cardiovascular disease, cancer and diabetes. The largest reduction, 10–20%, was at about 30–60 minutes a week. Combining strength with aerobic activity was associated with 40% lower mortality. This is an association, not proof of cause. Verified
  • Balance work reduces falls from age 60: High. Cochrane review 2019 (108 RCTs, 23,407 people, mean age 76): exercise reduced the rate of falls by 23%; balance and functional exercise by 24%. High-certainty evidence. There are no comparable data for adults aged 18–64. Verified
