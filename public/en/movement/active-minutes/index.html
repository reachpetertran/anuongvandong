How many minutes of activity a week

Checked: 2026-11-24Evidence: High (WHO grades it a strong recommendation)Ages: 18-39, 40-64Author: Peter Tran

Educational content, not yet reviewed by a health professional. It does not replace advice from a doctor who knows your health.

The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original. Quotes from Vietnamese documents are unofficial translations.

Key points

  • Adults should do 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, or a mix of both. Verified
  • Add muscle-strengthening on at least 2 days a week, working the major muscle groups. Verified
  • Sit less. Replace sitting time with activity of any intensity. Verified

What the standards say

Vietnam – Department of Disease Prevention, Ministry of Health (Decision 103/QĐ-PB, 13 Jul 2026), ages 18–59 150–300 min moderate (30–60 min on most days) or 75–150 min vigorous; at least 5 days a week At least 2 days a week, major muscle groups Limit sedentary time; replace it with activity of any intensity
WHO (2020) 150–300 min moderate or 75–150 min vigorous At least 2 days a week Limit sedentary time
US – PAG, second edition 150–300 min moderate At least 2 days a week –
Australia – 24-hour guidelines, ages 18–64 (updated 3 Jun 2026) Moderate to vigorous 30 min or more on most days; several hours of light activity daily At least 2 days a week; plus balance and coordination on at least 3 days a week Break up long sitting as often as possible
UK – NHS, ages 19–64 At least 150 min moderate or 75 min vigorous At least 2 days a week Sit less; break it up with activity

The standards almost match. Australia differs by adding balance work for ages 18–64 and by framing activity per day rather than per week. Verified

What counts as "moderate" and "vigorous"

  • Moderate: you breathe faster and can still say short sentences, but not sing. Examples: brisk walking, easy cycling. Inference
  • Vigorous: you are out of breath and can say only a few words. Examples: running, fast swimming. Inference
  • Vietnam's handbook uses brisk walking (about 4 km/h or faster) as an example of activity. Verified
  • Across standards, 1 minute of vigorous activity counts as roughly 2 minutes of moderate, which is why 150 minutes moderate pairs with 75 minutes vigorous. Inference

How to do it – the minimal version

Step 1. Pick 5 days a week. A 30-minute brisk walk each day gives 150 minutes. You can split it into three 10-minute walks. Inference

Step 2. Pick 2 strength days, not back to back. For example Tuesday and Friday. No equipment needed: sit-to-stand from a chair, wall push-ups, lifting water bottles. Inference

Step 3. Break up sitting. At a desk, stand up and walk for a few minutes every so often. The standards give no exact number of minutes. Verified

Step 4. Log one week. Add up the minutes at the weekend. If you fall short, build up gradually; you don't need to reach the target in week one. "Some activity is better than none" is a core message of WHO 2020. Verified

Hypothetical example: Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday and Sunday – 30-minute brisk walk (150 minutes). Tuesday and Friday – 20 minutes of strength work at home. Hypothetical example

Common misunderstandings

  • "It only counts if I do it all at once." The standards count total minutes over the week. Vietnam's handbook says 30–60 minutes on most days and does not require one continuous session. Inference
  • "Walking is enough; I don't need strength work." All five standards list strength work separately, at least 2 days a week. Verified
  • "More than 300 minutes is too much." Vietnam's handbook says you can go beyond 300 minutes moderate or 150 minutes vigorous for greater benefit. Verified

Who should take care

  • People with a long-term condition (heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, joint disease…): Vietnam's handbook advises considering a health check before starting. Verified
  • People who have been inactive for a long time: start at a level that matches your current fitness and build up. Verified
  • Pregnant women and new mothers: there are separate recommendations; this article does not apply. Ask a health worker. Inference
  • Exercising in the heat: drink enough water and avoid the hottest hours. Inference

When to see a health worker

  • Stop at once if you feel marked fatigue, dizziness, shortness of breath or sharp pain, especially chest pain. Seek care if symptoms get worse. Verified
  • Severe chest pain, fainting or serious breathlessness: call emergency services (see Find help).
  • You have a condition and don't know what level of activity is safe.

Level of evidence

High. The paper announcing the WHO 2020 guidelines grades the 150–300-minute and 2-day strength recommendations for adults as strong recommendations. Verified
