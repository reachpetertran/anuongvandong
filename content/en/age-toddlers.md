*The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.*

*This page only summarises official guidance. It does not replace check-ups. The site gives no micronutrient doses and names no products for children.*

## Key points

- Children aged 1–2 should **be active for at least 180 minutes a day**, at any intensity, spread through the day. Vietnam, WHO and Australia agree. [Verified]
- **Screens:** not encouraged at age 1; from age 2, no more than 1 hour a day. **Sleep 11–14 hours** in 24 hours, including naps. [Verified]
- **Keep breastfeeding** to 2 years or beyond. A varied diet with less seasoning; **no foods or drinks high in sugar or salt**. Special milks for older babies and toddlers are not needed (WHO, US). [Verified]

## What the standards say

**Activity and screens** [Verified]

| Standard | Activity | Recreational screen time |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnam – Decision 103/QĐ-PB (13 Jul 2026), 1 to under 3 | More is better; at least 180 minutes a day at any intensity, spread through the day, indoors and outdoors | Not encouraged at age 1; ages 2 to under 3 no more than 1 hour a day, less is better |
| WHO (2019), 1–2 years | At least 180 minutes at any intensity. Not restrained for more than 1 hour at a time | Age 1: not recommended. Age 2: no more than 1 hour |
| Australia – 24-hour guidelines | At least 3 hours of activity a day | Under 2: none. 2 and over: no more than 1 hour |
| US, UK | Not verified for ages 1–2 specifically | Not verified |

**Sleep hours (per 24 hours, including naps)** [Verified]

| Standard | 1–2 years |
|---|---|
| WHO (2019) | 11–14 hours |
| US – AASM (2016) | 11–14 hours |
| Australia – 24-hour guidelines | 11–14 hours |
| Vietnam | No sleep-hours recommendation verified |

**Food** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Vietnam – Ministry of Health (Decision 318/QĐ-BYT, 30 Jan 2026) | Continue breastfeeding to 24 months or longer. 12–23 months: 3 meals and 2 snacks a day, about ¾ to 1 small bowl per meal; each meal with all 4 food groups. Drinking water about 250–350 ml a day, not counting milk and water in food. Light seasoning; limit sweets, sugary drinks, cakes, crisps. Eat at the table with the family, no roaming meals, no TV or phone at meals. The NIN's 2025 set of 7 pyramids has no pyramid for children under 3 |
| WHO – Complementary feeding guideline (2023), 12–23 months | Continue breastfeeding. Meat, fish or eggs daily; fruit and vegetables daily; pulses, nuts and seeds often. No foods high in sugar, salt or trans fats; no sugar-sweetened drinks; no non-sugar sweeteners; limit 100% juice. Responsive feeding |
| US – DGA (12 months – 2 years) | Breastfeeding can continue. Stop infant formula at 12 months; switch to whole milk or unsweetened fortified soy milk. Toddler milks are not needed. Avoid drinks with caffeine or added sugar. Limit salty and processed foods |
| UK – NHS | Children aged 4 or under should avoid food and drinks with added sugar or sweeteners, including honey, syrup, juice and smoothies. No whole nuts before age 5 |

**What milk if not breastfed** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Vietnam – Ministry of Health (Decision 318/QĐ-BYT, 2026) | From 12 months, pasteurised animal milk or full-fat milk powder can be added; choose no-added-sugar or low-sugar products |
| WHO (2023), 12–23 months | Animal milk. Follow-up formula is not recommended |
| US – DGA, CDC | Pasteurised, unsweetened whole cow's milk, or unsweetened fortified soy milk |

**Juice – the standards differ slightly** [Verified]

- WHO: limit 100% juice. CDC: after 12 months, juice is unnecessary.
- DGA: optional; if given, 100% juice, up to about 120 ml a day; whole fruit preferred.
- NHS: children aged 4 or under should avoid juice and smoothies.

## How to do it – the minimal version

1. **Active play spread through the day:** walking, climbing, crawling, pushing toys, throwing a ball, dancing. Add up to 3 hours. [Verified]
2. **Don't keep the child still for more than 1 hour at a time** in a pushchair, high chair or carrier. [Verified – WHO]
3. **Screens:** none at age 1; under 1 hour a day at age 2. Read and tell stories instead. [Verified – Decision 103]
4. **Regular sleep times,** 11–14 hours including naps. [Verified for the hours]
5. **Share family meals, but take the child's portion out before seasoning.** Meat, fish or eggs, vegetables and fruit every day. [Verified for food groups – WHO; method: Inference]
6. **Water and unsweetened milk** instead of soft drinks and sweetened milk. See [Free sugars](/en/eating/free-sugars/). [Verified]
7. **Let the child decide how much to eat:** don't force, don't use sweets as rewards. [Verified for responsive feeding – WHO; rewards: Expert judgement]

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Over-1s need growing-up formula."** WHO: follow-up formula is not recommended. US: toddler milks are not needed. [Verified]
- **"A phone helps them eat."** Decision 103 and WHO do not encourage screens at age 1; at age 2 no more than 1 hour. [Verified]
- **"Fruit juice is a healthy drink."** WHO limits it, the NHS advises avoiding it for young children; whole fruit is better. [Verified]

## Who should take care

- Children with food allergies, underweight, overweight or slow growth: their diet needs individual advice from a health worker. The site gives no weight-loss advice for children. [Expert judgement]
- Children with heart disease, asthma, epilepsy or a physical disability: ask a doctor about activity levels. [Expert judgement]
- Vegan families: need a dietitian's advice. See [Plant-based eating](/en/methods/plant-based-eating/). [Verified]

## When to see a health worker

- Call **113** straight away if the child is choking and cannot breathe, has trouble breathing, turns blue, has a fit, is floppy, or has facial swelling and trouble breathing after eating. [Expert judgement]
- See a health worker soon if the child is not yet walking, has no single words, loses skills they had, snores loudly or stops breathing in sleep, or has not gained weight for months. [Expert judgement]
- A child facing abuse or violence: call **111** (Vietnam's national child protection hotline). See [Find help](/en/find-help/).

From age 3: see [Preschool (3–5 years)](/en/age/preschool/).
