*The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.*

*This page only summarises official guidance. It does not replace check-ups. The site gives no medicine or micronutrient doses and names no formula products.*

## Key points

- **Breastfeed within 1 hour of birth, breastfeed exclusively for the first 6 months**, then continue to 2 years or beyond. Vietnam's Ministry of Health, WHO, the US and Australia agree. [Verified]
- **Safe sleep:** always put the baby on their back, on a firm flat surface, with no pillows, loose blankets or soft toys; same room, own sleep space. [Verified]
- Babies need **14–17 hours of sleep** (0–3 months), **12–16 hours** (4–11 months). While awake: **at least 30 minutes of tummy time a day**, no screens. [Verified]

## What the standards say

**Breastfeeding** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Vietnam – Ministry of Health (Circular 38/2016/TT-BYT, in force 1 Jan 2017, Article 2; Decision 1982/QĐ-BYT, 1 Jul 2026) | Breastfeed within the first hour after birth; exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months; continue to 24 months or longer. No food or drink other than breast milk under 6 months unless medically indicated. Feed on demand. No bottles or artificial teats for breastfed babies. Mother and baby stay together 24 hours a day after birth |
| WHO (fact sheet updated 4 Aug 2026) | Breastfeed within 1 hour; only breast milk for the first 6 months; continue to 2 years or beyond, with complementary foods from 6 months |
| US – AAP (2022) and DGA | Breast milk only for about 6 months, no other food or drink, not even water. Without breast milk: iron-fortified infant formula; no home-made formula |
| Australia – NHMRC | Exclusive breastfeeding to around 6 months. Without breast milk, infant formula is the safest alternative under 12 months. No animal milk as the main drink before 12 months |
| UK – NHS | Breast milk or first infant formula is the main nutrition; solids from around 6 months |

The US also advises a vitamin D supplement for breastfed babies from birth. A health worker sets the dose; the site gives none. [Verified for the recommendation – DGA]

**Sleep hours (per 24 hours, including daytime sleep)** [Verified]

| Standard | 0–3 months | 4–11 months |
|---|---|---|
| WHO (2019) | 14–17 hours | 12–16 hours |
| US – AASM (2016) | No recommendation | 12–16 hours (4–12 months) |
| Australia – 24-hour guidelines | 14–17 hours | 12–16 hours (4–12 months) |
| Vietnam | Not verified | Not verified |

**Safe sleep** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| US – AAP (2022) | On the back for every sleep. Firm, flat, non-inclined surface with nothing soft. Room-share without bed-sharing for at least the first 6 months. If the baby falls asleep in a car seat, stroller or swing, move them to a flat sleep surface as soon as possible |
| UK – NHS (reviewed 25 Nov 2025) | On the back, feet at the bottom of the cot. Firm flat mattress; no pillows, duvets, soft toys or bumpers. Own cot in the parents' room for at least 6 months. Never sleep with the baby on a sofa. Don't let the baby overheat. No smoking near the baby |
| Australia – Red Nose, state health departments | 6 steps: on the back; head and face uncovered; smoke-free before and after birth; safe sleep space day and night; own cot in an adult's room for the first 6–12 months; breastfeed |
| Vietnam | No official Ministry of Health guidance verified. A Vietnamese-language reference: *Safer Sleep for Babies* (Scottish Government and NHS Scotland, 2024, Vietnamese edition) |

The NHS lists when **not to share a bed** with a baby: born before 37 weeks or under 2.5 kg; a parent smokes, has drunk alcohol, used drugs or taken medicine that causes drowsiness. The AAP does not support bed-sharing under any circumstances. [Verified]

**Activity and screens** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Vietnam – Decision 103/QĐ-PB (2026), under 1 year | Active several times a day on the floor or bed; at least 30 minutes a day; tummy time, crawling, learning to walk |
| WHO (2019), under 1 year | Active several times a day; babies not yet mobile: at least 30 minutes on the tummy, spread through waking time. Not restrained for more than 1 hour at a time. No screen time |
| Australia – 24-hour guidelines | No screen time under 2 years |

## How to do it – the minimal version

1. **Feed on demand,** day and night. [Verified – Ministry of Health, Circular 38/2016] No water, sugar water or honey. [Verified – WHO, DGA, CDC]
2. **Every sleep: on the back,** in a cot or bassinet of their own next to the parents' bed. Remove pillows, thick blankets and soft toys. [Verified]
3. **Dress warmly enough but not too warm,** head uncovered for sleep. [Verified – NHS, Red Nose]
4. **Tummy time while awake,** with an adult watching, adding up to at least 30 minutes a day. [Verified]
5. **Turn off the TV and phone** around the baby. [Verified – WHO]
6. **No smoking** in the home or near the baby. [Verified]
7. **Keep to the vaccination and check-up schedule** of your health facility. [Expert judgement]

## Common misunderstandings

- **"In hot weather babies need extra water."** WHO and the US: babies under 6 months need only breast milk, no water unless medically needed. [Verified]
- **"A dab of honey is fine."** US (CDC): no honey before 12 months, not even on a dummy; it can cause botulism. [Verified]
- **"Babies sleep better on their tummy."** AAP, NHS, Australia: always on the back for sleep. Tummy time only while awake and watched. [Verified]

## Who should take care

- Babies born early or small, or with heart or lung problems or prolonged jaundice: follow the doctor's own instructions. [Expert judgement]
- Mothers taking medicines, with a long-term condition, or having trouble breastfeeding: ask a health worker; don't stop breastfeeding on your own. [Expert judgement]
- Babies not breastfed: ask a health worker about the type of milk and safe preparation. The site names no products. [Expert judgement]

## When to see a health worker

Take the baby to a health facility **straight away** or call **113** if the baby: [Expert judgement]

- Stops feeding or feeds very poorly, is floppy or hard to wake.
- Has a fever, especially under 3 months; or feels unusually cold.
- Breathes fast, the chest sucks in, lips turn blue, stops breathing for spells, or has a fit.
- Vomits repeatedly or brings up green fluid; has a lot of diarrhoea; has far fewer wet nappies.
- Has jaundice on the first day, or jaundice spreading quickly to the arms and legs.

A baby facing violence or neglect: call **111** (Vietnam's national child protection hotline). See [Find help](/en/find-help/).
