## Key points

- Menopause is the **final menstrual period**. You only know for sure after **12 months in a row** without a period and no other cause. Most women reach menopause between **45 and 55**. [Verified]
- Symptoms such as hot flushes, night sweats and poor sleep can start years before periods stop (perimenopause) and last years afterwards. [Verified]
- Several things can ease symptoms: lifestyle changes, psychological therapy, and medicines with or without hormones. Which to choose should be **discussed with a doctor**, because benefits and risks differ from person to person. [Verified]

## What the standards say

| Standard | Main recommendations |
|---|---|
| WHO (fact sheet, 16 Oct 2024) | Most women reach menopause between 45 and 55. Natural menopause is counted after 12 consecutive months without a period. Before 40 is premature menopause. Perimenopause can last several years. Both non-hormonal and hormonal interventions can ease symptoms. Bone loss at menopause contributes to higher osteoporosis and fracture rates. Pregnancy is still possible in perimenopause; contraception is advised until 12 months without a period. |
| Vietnam – Ministry of Health | In December 2024 the Ministry issued *National guidance on reproductive health services – Care for women in perimenopause and menopause*. The site has not been able to read the full text, so its content is not summarised here (not verified). |
| US – FDA, Office on Women's Health (OWH) | Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT) "is not for everyone" and has benefits and risks; if used, use the lowest dose that helps for the shortest time needed. OWH: hot flushes are the most common symptom, affecting up to 3 in 4 women. |
| Australia – Department of Health (2026) | Average age at menopause is 51. Before 45 is early menopause; before 40 is premature ovarian insufficiency (POI). About 1 in 15 Australian women have POI or early menopause. Current Australian evidence shows MHT is safe and effective for most healthy people for hot flushes, night sweats and vaginal dryness. |
| UK – NHS, NICE (NG23, updated 15 Apr 2026) | No routine test; a doctor may diagnose from age and symptoms. Menopause before 45 needs hormone replacement because of higher risks to bones and heart. NICE (2024): CBT helps with hot flushes, night sweats, depressive symptoms and sleep problems, alongside or instead of hormones. |

The standards differ in **how they talk about hormone therapy**: the US (FDA) stresses lowest dose and shortest time; Australia says it is safe and effective for most healthy people; the UK (NICE) stresses discussing benefits and risks person by person and reviewing at every follow-up. The site does not take sides; the decision is yours and your doctor's. [Verified]

## How to do it – minimal version

1. **Keep a record** of your periods and symptoms (hot flushes, sleep, mood) for a few months. Take it to your appointment. [Inference]
2. **Lifestyle** (Australian Department of Health): stop smoking and vaping; limit alcohol and caffeine; eat well; stay active; keep regular sleep times; wear layers you can take off during a hot flush. [Verified]
3. **During hot flushes:** OWH (US) suggests noting triggers (spicy food, alcohol, caffeine, stress, hot places) and avoiding them; slow, deep breaths when a flush starts may make it shorter. See [Five minutes of slow breathing](/en/breathing/slow-breathing/). [Verified]
4. **Keep bone and muscle:** weight-bearing exercise, 2 strength sessions a week, balance training. See [Strength and balance](/en/movement/strength-balance/). [Verified]
5. **If symptoms affect your life:** see a doctor to discuss options (CBT, medicines with or without hormones). Do not buy hormones and use them on your own. [Expert judgement]

## Common misconceptions

- **"A few months without a period means menopause, so no more contraception."** WHO: pregnancy is still possible in perimenopause; contraception is advised until 12 months in a row without a period. [Verified]
- **"You need a blood test to know it's menopause."** NHS: there is no routine test; doctors usually diagnose from age and symptoms. Under 45, a doctor may order tests. [Verified]
- **"'Natural' products are safe."** Australian Department of Health: "natural" does not mean safe; herbs and supplements can interact with medicines, and most lack strong evidence. [Verified]
- **"Menopause is a disease."** WHO: menopause is a natural part of biological ageing. [Verified]

## Who should take care

- People who have had breast cancer, blood clots, heart disease or stroke: treatment options differ; always ask a doctor first. [Expert judgement]
- People taking medicines: tell your doctor about any herbs or supplements you use. [Verified]
- People whose periods stopped before 45, or after removal of the ovaries, chemotherapy or radiotherapy: should be assessed separately. [Verified]
- This article does not give medicine instructions, doses or product names.

## When to see a health worker

- Periods stop, or menopause symptoms start, **before 45**. [Verified]
- Symptoms affect your work, sleep or daily life, or worry you. [Verified]
- Periods come very close together, last more than a week, are heavy, or **bleeding returns after a year without periods** (MedlinePlus, US). [Verified]
- Lasting low mood, loss of interest, thoughts of harming yourself: get help now (see [Find help](/en/find-help/)).

## Level of evidence

- **Definition, age range: High** – WHO, NHS and Australian guidance agree. [Verified]
- **CBT for hot flushes, sleep problems, depressive symptoms: Moderate** – NICE reviewed the evidence in 2024 and recommends it. [Verified]
- **Hormone therapy:** its effect on hot flushes is recognised by the standards; the balance of benefits and risks depends on age, timing, type of medicine and personal history. The site does not judge this in place of a doctor. [Expert judgement]
