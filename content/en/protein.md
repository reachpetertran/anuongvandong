*The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original. Quotes from Vietnamese documents are unofficial translations.*

## Key points

- Recommended protein for healthy adults: **WHO 0.83 g/kg/day**, **US (RDA) 0.8 g/kg**, **UK 0.75 g/kg**, Australia 46–64 g/day. The WHO level covers about 97.5% of healthy adults. [Verified]
- The US **DGA 2025–2030** sets a goal of **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day**. This figure is being debated by nutrition scientists. [Verified]
- Vietnam's Ministry of Health advises **consuming protein-rich foods appropriately**: fish, poultry and nuts daily, and limiting red meat. [Verified]

## What the standards say

| Standard | Healthy adults | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnam – Decision 3594/QĐ-BYT (2024), recommendation 3 | No g/kg figure | Fish, poultry and nuts daily; limit red meat. The *Recommended Dietary Allowances for Vietnamese people* table gives protein figures, but the site **has not checked the original yet**, so no number is published. |
| WHO/FAO/UNU (2007) | **0.83 g/kg/day** (safe level) | Covers 97.5% of healthy adults. No upper limit identified; intakes of twice this level are unlikely to carry risk |
| US – NASEM, DRI (2005) | RDA **0.8 g/kg**; men 56 g, women 46 g a day at reference body weight | Still the US nutrient reference value |
| US – DGA 2025–2030 (p. 3) | Goal of **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day**, adjusted to energy needs | Prioritise protein at every meal; vary animal and plant sources |
| Australia – NHMRC (2006) | Men 19–70: **64 g/day**; women 19–70: **46 g/day** (0.75 g/kg) | Higher over 70: men 81 g, women 57 g |
| UK – PHE (2016), RNI | **0.75 g/kg**; men 19–64: 56 g, women: 45 g a day | – |

**Debate:** a Perspective in *The Journal of Nutrition* (American Society for Nutrition, 2026) examines whether the evidence is enough to recommend higher protein for **all** Americans. [Verified]

## Work out your needs

Multiply **body weight (kg) × the g/kg level** of the standard you choose. [Inference]

| Body weight | WHO (0.83 g/kg) | UK (0.75 g/kg) | US DGA (1.2–1.6 g/kg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 kg | about 42 g | about 38 g | 60–80 g |
| 60 kg | about 50 g | 45 g | 72–96 g |
| 70 kg | about 58 g | about 53 g | 84–112 g |

## How to do it – the minimal version

**Step 1. Include a protein source at each main meal.** Fish, poultry, eggs, tofu, beans, peanuts, sesame. [Verified for the foods; the meal split is Inference]

**Step 2. Swap some red meat for fish, chicken or beans.** This is the Ministry of Health's direction. [Verified]

**Step 3. Combine plant proteins.** Rice with tofu, bean soup, vegetables stir-fried with peanuts. The DGA also lists beans, lentils, nuts, seeds and soy as protein sources. [Verified]

**Step 4. Choose low-oil cooking.** The DGA suggests swapping deep-frying for baking, roasting, stir-frying or grilling. [Verified]

**Step 5. You don't need protein powder if you eat normally.** Ordinary food can meet the levels above. [Expert judgement]

## Common misunderstandings

- **"The more protein, the better."** WHO set no upper limit, but the recommended levels already meet the needs of most healthy people. Extra protein does not replace activity and strength training. [Verified for WHO; the rest is Expert judgement]
- **"The US says 1.2–1.6 g/kg, so everyone must eat that."** WHO, Australia and the UK keep about 0.75–0.83 g/kg; the US itself still has an RDA of 0.8 g/kg. The new DGA figure is debated. [Verified]
- **"Plant protein isn't enough."** A diet with a variety of plant proteins can meet needs; vegans need to watch vitamin B12. See the plant-based eating article (coming soon). [Expert judgement]

## Who should take care

- **People with kidney disease:** do not increase protein on your own. The British Heart Foundation notes that regularly eating more than 2 g/kg can strain the kidneys, especially with kidney problems. Ask your doctor. [Verified]
- **Older adults:** Australia sets higher levels over 70. Ask a health worker about your needs; the site will have a separate article for this group. [Verified]
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding women, children, athletes:** needs differ; this article does not apply. [Inference]
- **People with gout, liver disease or diabetes:** ask a health worker before big diet changes. [Expert judgement]

## When to see a health worker

- You have kidney or liver disease or gout, or follow a therapeutic diet. [Expert judgement]
- Unexplained weight loss or muscle weakness, especially in older adults. [Expert judgement]
- You plan to use protein powders or supplements long term. [Expert judgement]

## Level of evidence

- **Minimum adequate level (0.75–0.83 g/kg): Moderate.** Based on analysis of nitrogen balance studies (WHO/FAO/UNU 2007). [Verified]
- **Advising everyone to eat 1.2–1.6 g/kg: Low to moderate.** There is evidence for some groups (weight management, older adults), but no agreement for the whole population. [Expert judgement]
