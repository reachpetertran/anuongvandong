## Key points

- **Sleep:** 7–9 hours with consistent bed and wake-up times. This is Australia's official recommendation (2026) and a US expert consensus (AASM/SRS 2015: 7 hours or more). [Verified]
- **5 minutes of slow breathing** and **10 minutes of mindfulness** are two minimal ways to manage stress. The evidence is low to moderate, mostly for short-term effects. [Verified]
- This article combines 3 detailed articles into **one daily plan**. Each part has an evidence card and a list of who should not use it. [Inference]

## How to do it – a daily plan

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Get up at a fixed time, weekends included | – |
| Morning or lunch | Breath-awareness mindfulness | 10 minutes |
| Early afternoon | Last coffee (if you sleep at 10 pm) | before about 1 pm |
| Evening | No alcohol in the 4 hours before bed; put the phone away | – |
| Before bed | Slow breathing: in for a count of 5, out for a count of 5 | 5 minutes |

[Hypothetical example] – adjust the times to your working day.

**How to start:** in week 1, keep only the **fixed wake-up time**. In week 2, add slow breathing. In week 3, add mindfulness. Adding one thing at a time makes it easier to keep going. [Inference]

**Simple tracking:** at the end of each week, tick how many days you did each part, and note how alert you felt during the day. [Inference]

## Evidence card

| Part | Level of evidence | Main basis |
|---|---|---|
| 7+ hours of sleep, regularly | **Moderate to high** | AASM/SRS consensus after a literature review; Australia grades the sleep-duration recommendation as strong with low-certainty evidence. Links between short sleep and disease come mainly from observational data. |
| 5 minutes of slow breathing | **Low to moderate** | Fincham 2023: breathwork reduced stress by a small-to-moderate amount (12 RCTs, moderate risk of bias). Shao 2024: systolic blood pressure fell right after practice; long-term effects unclear. |
| 10 minutes of mindfulness | **Moderate** for 8-week programmes; **no specific evidence** for 10 minutes of self-practice | Goyal 2014: small-to-moderate improvements in anxiety, depression and pain. Farias 2020: about 8% had unwanted effects. |

[Verified]

## Common misunderstandings

- **"These 3 habits will cure insomnia or anxiety."** They are preventive habits for healthy people. Chronic insomnia needs CBT-I; lasting anxiety or depression needs a health worker. [Verified for CBT-I; the rest is Expert judgement]
- **"You must do all 3 to benefit."** Each part has its own basis. Doing one is better than none. [Inference]
- **"The longer you meditate or breathe, the better."** There is no evidence on the best dose. 5 and 10 minutes are easy starting points. [Inference]

## Who should take care

- **Sleep:** night and shift workers; people with insomnia should not try "sleep restriction" on their own. [Expert judgement]
- **Breathing:** people with lung or heart conditions should ask a health worker first; never over-breathe and then hold your breath in or near water. [Verified]
- **Meditation:** if you have thoughts of self-harm, are currently harming yourself or have had a recent manic episode, mindfulness should not be your only way of coping; with PTSD, mindfulness could make symptoms worse. [Verified]
- **Stop meditating** if anxiety clearly increases, your mood drops, painful memories flood back, or you feel detached from reality. [Expert judgement]
- **Do not stop medicines** (blood pressure, psychiatric, sleep) because you are doing breathing or meditation. [Expert judgement]

## When to see a health worker

- Trouble sleeping that goes on, especially for 3 months or longer. [Verified]
- Loud snoring, pauses in breathing during sleep, heavy daytime sleepiness. [Expert judgement]
- Low mood or anxiety lasting more than 2 weeks. [Expert judgement]
- **Thoughts of suicide or self-harm: seek help now** (see [Find help](/en/find-help/)).
