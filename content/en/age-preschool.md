*The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.*

## Key points

- Children aged 3–5 should **be active for at least 180 minutes a day**, including at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity. Vietnam and WHO agree on this figure. [Verified]
- **Recreational screen time no more than 1 hour a day**, less is better; **10–13 hours of sleep** in 24 hours, including naps. [Verified]
- Vietnam's National Institute of Nutrition has a **separate food pyramid for 3–5-year-olds**, with less salt and sugar than for adults. [Verified]

Age bands: Vietnam (Decision 103) and WHO use "3 to under 5"; from 5, children follow the 5–17 advice. Australia, the US and AASM use 3–5. [Verified]

## What the standards say

**Activity and screens** [Verified]

| Standard | Activity | Recreational screen time |
|---|---|---|
| Vietnam – Department of Disease Prevention (Decision 103/QĐ-PB, 13 Jul 2026), 3 to under 5 | At least 180 minutes a day, varied activities through the day, indoors and outdoors; at least 60 minutes moderate and vigorous. Don't leave children sitting or lying long in prams or fixed seats | No more than 1 hour a day, less is better; replace with reading and storytelling |
| WHO (2019), 3–4 years | At least 180 minutes at any intensity, of which at least 60 minutes moderate to vigorous, spread through the day; more is better | No more than 1 hour a day (ages 2–4) |
| US – PAG/CDC, 3–5 years | Active throughout the day; caregivers should encourage active play, aiming for at least 3 hours a day | Not verified |
| Australia – 24-Hour Guidelines (0–5) | At least 3 hours of activity a day; don't restrain for more than 1 hour at a time | No more than 1 hour a day |
| UK – CMOs (updated 2026), under 5 | At least 180 minutes a day, including at least 60 minutes moderate to vigorous | No hour limit given |

**Sleep (per 24 hours, including naps)** [Verified]

| Standard | 3–5 years |
|---|---|
| WHO (2019), 3–4 years | 10–13 hours |
| US – AASM (2016, endorsed by AAP) | 10–13 hours |
| Australia – 24-Hour Guidelines | 10–13 hours; some will still need naps |
| Vietnam | No sleep-hours recommendation verified |

## Eating: food pyramid for children aged 3–5

**National Institute of Nutrition, Decision 640/QĐ-VDD (26 May 2025) – average for 1 child per day** [Verified]

| Food group | Children 3–5 | 1 unit equals |
|---|---|---|
| Grains, tubers (eat enough) | 5–6 units | 20 g carbohydrate |
| Vegetables (eat enough) | 2 units | 80 g |
| Ripe fruit (eat enough) | 2 units | 80 g |
| Meat, seafood, eggs, protein-rich seeds (eat enough) | 3.5 units | 7 g protein |
| Milk and dairy (eat enough) | 4 units | 100 mg calcium |
| Oils, fats, oily seeds (limit) | 5 units | 5 g |
| Sugar, sweets (limit) | under 3 units | 5 g |
| Salt (limit) | under 3 units | 1 g |
| Drinking water | 6 units | 200 ml |

The pyramid also says: **physical activity at least 60 minutes a day**. The site built this table from the figures and does not reproduce the pyramid image. How to read the pyramid: see [The plate and the food pyramid](/en/eating/plate-and-pyramid/). [Verified]

## How to do it – minimal version

1. **Let children play actively throughout the day:** running, jumping, safe climbing, throwing and catching, tricycles. Add up to 3 hours. [Verified for the example activities – Decision 103]
2. **Don't keep children sitting still for more than 1 hour at a time** in prams, high chairs or car seats. [Verified – Australia]
3. **Recreational screens under 1 hour a day.** Replace with reading and storytelling together. [Verified]
4. **Keep sleep times regular**, 10–13 hours including naps. [Verified for the hours]
5. **Season children's food more lightly than adults':** the 3–5 pyramid sets salt under 3 g and sugar under 15 g a day (under 3 units × 5 g). [Verified]
6. **Water instead of sugary drinks.** See [Free sugars](/en/eating/free-sugars/). [Verified]

## Common misconceptions

- **"Lots of running around tires them out, so let them rest in front of the TV."** The standards say more activity is better at this age, and limit recreational screens. [Verified]
- **"They can just eat what adults eat."** The 3–5 pyramid sets lower salt and sugar than for adults (under 3 versus under 5 units). [Verified]

## Who should take care

- Children with heart disease, asthma, epilepsy, a physical disability or under treatment: ask a doctor about suitable activity. [Expert judgement]
- Children with food allergies, underweight or overweight, or long-term poor appetite: diet needs individual advice from a health worker. The site gives no weight-loss advice for children. [Expert judgement]
- Children under 3: no separate page yet; the under-2 group is a high-risk topic. [Verified]

## When to see a health worker

- Delayed speech or movement compared with peers; loud snoring or pauses in breathing during sleep. [Expert judgement]
- Poor appetite, weight loss or no weight gain for several months. [Expert judgement]
- Difficulty breathing, turning blue or fainting during play: **call emergency services** (see [Find help](/en/find-help/)). Abuse or violence against a child: call **111** (Vietnam's national child protection hotline).
