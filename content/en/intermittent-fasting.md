## Key points

- Intermittent fasting sets rules on **when** you eat or on **low-intake days**, not on what you eat. Three common types: 16:8, 5:2, alternate-day fasting. [Verified]
- Compared with regular dietary advice, intermittent fasting **may make little to no difference** to weight loss (Cochrane 2026). [Verified]
- **It is not safe for everyone.** Healthdirect (Australian Government) lists groups who should not do it – see "Who should not" below. [Verified]

## Who should not do intermittent fasting

According to Healthdirect (Australia, Dec 2025), you **should not** do intermittent fasting if you: [Verified]

- are underweight;
- have, or have had, an eating disorder;
- are pregnant or breastfeeding;
- are a child or teenager;
- are an older adult;
- take daily medicine that must be taken with food.

**Ask your doctor first:** people having cancer treatment; people taking diabetes medicines (Healthdirect, Diabetes UK). [Verified]

The site adds (not in the official sources): people with kidney disease, gout or a history of fainting, heavy manual workers and night-shift workers should ask a health worker first. [Expert judgement]

## How to do it – minimal version

Only for healthy adults who are not in the groups above.

1. **See a doctor first** if you take regular medicines (Healthdirect). [Verified]
2. **Start gently:** a 12-hour eating window (e.g. 7 am–7 pm) for 2–4 weeks. If easy, narrow to 10 hours. [Inference]
3. **Put the eating window in the daytime**, avoid late-night eating. [Inference]
4. **Within the window, eat normally and well:** vegetables at meals, enough protein, few sugary drinks. Healthdirect: no foods are required or banned; drink water at any time, including while fasting. See [The plate and the food pyramid](/en/eating/plate-and-pyramid/). [Verified]
5. **Stop** if you feel weak, anxious or irritable, can't concentrate or can't manage usual activities (Healthdirect). [Verified]
6. **Review after about 12 weeks:** can you keep it up, are you overeating afterwards? A pattern you can sustain matters more than the clock. [Inference]

The site does not give personal calorie or weight targets.

## Common misconceptions

- **"Intermittent fasting works better for weight loss."** Cochrane 2026 and BMJ 2025 found no meaningful difference from ordinary dieting. [Verified]
- **"In the eating window, anything goes."** Healthdirect advises focusing on nutritious foods while eating. [Verified]
- **"Early hunger and headaches mean danger."** Healthdirect: hunger and headaches are common at first and usually ease. But weakness, anxiety or inability to concentrate mean stop and ask a doctor. [Verified]

## Who should take care

See **"Who should not do intermittent fasting"** above. Also:

- People with eating or weight concerns, or who worry a lot about food: fasting can make problems worse. [Expert judgement]
- People who fast for religious reasons and have a long-term condition: ask a health worker how to manage medicines during the fast (Diabetes UK). [Verified]

## When to see a health worker

- Before starting, if you take regular medicines. [Verified]
- Dizziness, shaking, sweating, fainting; weakness, anxiety, unable to manage usual activities. [Verified]
- Bingeing followed by self-blame, or thoughts about food taking up most of your time. [Expert judgement]
- Emergencies: see [Find help](/en/find-help/).

## Level of evidence

- **Weight loss similar to ordinary dieting: Moderate.** Two systematic reviews of RCTs agree; most trials were short (≤12 months) and small. [Verified]
- **Other benefits beyond weight loss (metabolic, longevity): Low.** Cochrane notes unwanted effects were reported inconsistently; longer trials are needed. [Expert judgement]
