## Key points

- **Myth:** intermittent fasting (16:8, 5:2, alternate-day…) leads to more weight loss than ordinary dieting. [Verified as a common myth]
- **Evidence:** Cochrane review 2026 (22 RCTs, 1,995 adults with overweight or obesity): compared with regular dietary advice, intermittent fasting may make **little to no difference** to weight loss. [Verified]
- BMJ review 2025 (99 RCTs, 6,582 adults): all intermittent fasting and continuous energy restriction diets reduced weight compared with unrestricted eating; only alternate-day fasting beat continuous restriction, by **1.29 kg**, below the 2 kg clinical threshold set by the authors. [Verified]

## What the standards say

| Source | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Cochrane (CD015610, 16 Feb 2026) | Versus regular dietary advice: may make little to no difference to weight loss. Versus no intervention: likely little to no difference. Unwanted effects (fatigue, headache, nausea) were reported inconsistently, so no firm conclusion. Most studies were in high-income countries. |
| BMJ 2025 (Semnani-Azad et al.; network meta-analysis) | All intermittent fasting and continuous restriction diets gave small weight losses versus unrestricted eating. Alternate-day fasting had a 1.29 kg edge that did not reach clinical significance; longer trials are needed. Trials lasted 3–52 weeks (average 12). |
| WHO, Vietnam MoH, US, Australia, UK | The site has not found official recommendations on intermittent fasting (not verified). |

## How to do it – minimal version

1. If you want to try it, know that **an eating pattern you can keep up** matters more than the clock. [Inference]
2. Whatever the timing, keep the basics: plenty of vegetables, enough protein, less salt, fewer sugary drinks. See [Salt](/en/eating/salt/), [Free sugars](/en/eating/free-sugars/), [Protein](/en/eating/protein/). [Verified]
3. Stop and ask a health worker if you feel dizzy, very tired, binge afterwards, or thoughts about food take over. [Expert judgement]

## Common misconceptions

- **"Fasting 'burns fat' better, so weight comes off faster."** The two reviews found no meaningful difference from ordinary dieting. [Verified]
- **"It's been proven not to work."** Not true: intermittent fasting **does reduce weight compared with unrestricted eating** (BMJ 2025). What is not proven is that it is better. [Verified]

## Who should take care

Healthdirect's (Australia) official list is in [Intermittent fasting](/en/methods/intermittent-fasting/). These groups **should not try it on their own** without asking a health worker: [Expert judgement]

- Under 18; pregnant or breastfeeding.
- Past or current eating disorder.
- Diabetes on glucose-lowering medicines; anyone who must take medicines with food.
- Frail older adults, unintended weight loss; kidney disease, gout, a history of fainting.
- Heavy physical work, night shifts.

## When to see a health worker

- Dizziness, shaking, sweating or fainting while fasting. [Expert judgement]
- Food becomes a constant worry; bingeing followed by self-blame. [Expert judgement]
- Emergencies: see [Find help](/en/find-help/).

## Level of evidence

- **Intermittent fasting is not better than ordinary dieting for weight loss: Moderate.** Two systematic reviews of RCTs agree, but most trials were short (≤12 months) and small. [Verified]
