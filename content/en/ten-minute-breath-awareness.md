## Key points

- **Mindfulness** means paying attention, moment by moment, to what is happening inside and around you, without judging. The basic practice: sit still, notice the breath, and when the mind wanders, gently come back. [Verified]
- Mindfulness **programmes** (usually 8 weeks, with a teacher) are associated with **small-to-moderate** reductions in anxiety, depression and pain. There is no specific evidence for 10 minutes of self-practice a day. [Verified]
- About **8% of people who meditate** have an unwanted effect, most often anxiety and depression. That is why this article includes **4 signs that you should stop**. [Verified]

This article teaches **secular** mindfulness for healthy adults. It is not tied to any religion and is not for self-treating an illness. [Inference]

## How to do it – the minimal version

**Step 1. Pick a regular time.** The NHS suggests a regular time of day. For example early morning or before bed. [Verified]

**Step 2. Sit comfortably and set a 10-minute timer.** Use a chair or cushion, back naturally upright. Eyes closed or looking at the floor. Ten minutes is an easy starting point; no standard sets a number. [Inference]

**Step 3. Notice the breath.** Feel it at the nose or belly. No need to control it; let it be natural. [Verified]

**Step 4. When the mind wanders, name it and come back.** Thinking of other things is normal. Notice, perhaps silently name it ("worrying"), then gently bring attention back to the breath. [Verified]

**Step 5. Finish gently.** Open your eyes, move your hands and feet, sit for a few seconds before standing. [Inference]

**Step 6. Practise mindfulness in daily tasks.** Notice the taste of food as you eat, the feel of your steps as you walk. [Verified]

> Hypothetical example: at 6 am, sit on a chair by the window, set a 10-minute timer and notice the breath at the belly. If the mind wanders 15 times, come back 15 times. [Hypothetical example]

## 4 signs that you should stop

Stop the practice and rest for a few days. If things do not improve, see a health worker. [Expert judgement]

1. **Anxiety clearly increases** during or after practice, or you have a panic attack.
2. **Your mood drops**, and you feel lower than before you started.
3. **Painful memories flood back** and are hard to control.
4. **Strange or detached feelings** from yourself or reality; or poor sleep that lasts after you start practising.

The Farias 2020 review found that unwanted effects can happen even in people **with no previous mental health problems**. [Verified]

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Meditation means emptying the mind."** The NHS describes mindfulness as watching thoughts as passing events, not trying to stop them. [Verified]
- **"Meditation only helps and never harms."** About 8% of people have an unwanted effect; this is similar to psychological therapies. [Verified]
- **"Meditation can replace depression treatment."** In NICE, group mindfulness is one option for less severe depression, considered with a health professional. MBCT (8 weeks) helps prevent relapse in people who have recovered. Do not stop medicines or treatment on your own. [Verified]
- **"Meditation beats everything else."** The Goyal 2014 review found no evidence that meditation was better than other active treatments such as drugs, exercise or behavioural therapies. [Verified]

## Who should take care

- **If you have thoughts of self-harm, are currently harming yourself, or have had a recent manic episode:** mindfulness should not be your only way of coping. Seek help now (see [Find help](/en/find-help/)). [Verified]
- **People with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD):** mindfulness could make trauma symptoms worse. [Verified] Practise with a qualified teacher. [Expert judgement]
- **People being treated for a mental health condition** (depression, anxiety, psychosis…): ask your treating doctor before practising on your own. [Expert judgement]
- **People with breathing problems or who find it hard to sit still:** breath-focused or sitting practice may not suit you; try mindful walking. [Verified]

## When to see a health worker

- You have one of the 4 signs above and it does not improve after stopping for a few days. [Expert judgement]
- Low mood or anxiety lasting more than 2 weeks and affecting work or sleep. [Expert judgement]
- **Thoughts of suicide or self-harm: seek help now** – go to the nearest health facility or call emergency services (see [Find help](/en/find-help/)).

## Level of evidence

- **Mindfulness programmes for anxiety, depression, pain: Moderate.** Goyal 2014 (JAMA Internal Medicine, 47 RCTs, over 3,500 people): moderate evidence of improved anxiety (effect size 0.38 at 8 weeks), depression (0.30) and pain (0.33). Low or insufficient evidence for positive mood, attention, sleep and weight. [Verified]
- **MBCT to prevent depressive relapse: Moderate to high.** Kuyken 2016 (JAMA Psychiatry, 9 RCTs, 1,258 people): people who took MBCT had a lower risk of relapse over 60 weeks (HR 0.69; 95% CI 0.58–0.82). This is an 8-week guided programme, not self-practice. [Verified]
- **Unwanted effects:** Farias 2020 (83 studies, 6,703 people): overall rate 8.3%; 3.7% in experimental studies, 33.2% in observational studies. [Verified]
