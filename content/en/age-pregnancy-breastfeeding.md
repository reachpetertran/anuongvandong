*The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original.*

*This page only summarises official guidance. It does not replace antenatal care. The site gives no medicine or micronutrient doses.*

## Key points

- Vietnam's National Institute of Nutrition (NIN) has a **separate food pyramid** for the first, second and third trimesters and for breastfeeding. Protein, dairy, grains and water rise step by step; salt and sugar stay under 5 units a day. [Verified]
- If there is no medical reason not to: **at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week** through pregnancy and after birth. Vietnam (Decision 103) and WHO agree on this figure. [Verified]
- **No alcohol** in pregnancy. Limit caffeine; avoid raw meat, eggs and seafood, and unpasteurised milk. **Go to antenatal checks.** [Verified]

## What the standards say

**Food: pyramid for pregnant and breastfeeding women**

NIN, Decision 640/QĐ-VDD (26 May 2025). Average for one person per day, in food units. "1–3 m" = months 1–3 of pregnancy; "Nursing" = breastfeeding. [Verified]

| Group | 1–3 m | 4–6 m | 7–9 m | Nursing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grains, roots | 12 | 13 | 13.5 | 14.5 |
| Vegetables | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Fruit | 3 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Meat, seafood, eggs, protein-rich seeds | 5 | 6 | 8 | 7 |
| Milk and dairy | 3 | 5 | 6 | 6.5 |
| Oils, fats, oily nuts | 5 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
| Sugar, sweets | &lt;5 | &lt;5 | &lt;5 | &lt;5 |
| Salt | &lt;5 | &lt;5 | &lt;5 | &lt;5 |
| Drinking water | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 |

**1 unit equals:** grains 20 g carbohydrate; vegetables 80 g; fruit 80 g; protein foods 7 g protein; dairy 100 mg calcium; oils 5 g; sugar 5 g; salt 1 g; water 200 ml. [Verified]

The pyramid also says: **light and moderate physical activity, 60 minutes a day**, and drink enough water every day. The site built this table from the figures and does not reproduce the pyramid image. How to read it: see [The plate and the pyramid](/en/eating/plate-and-pyramid/). [Verified]

**Activity** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Vietnam – Department of Disease Prevention (Decision 103/QĐ-PB, 13 Jul 2026, section 2.5) | Stay active through pregnancy and after birth. At least 150 minutes a week of moderate intensity. Add muscle-strengthening and gentle stretching; pelvic floor exercises daily. After birth, start small and build up |
| Vietnam – NIN pyramid (2025) | Light and moderate activity 60 minutes a day |
| WHO (2020) | Without contraindication: at least 150 minutes a week of moderate intensity, with aerobic and muscle-strengthening activity |
| US – CDC | At least 150 minutes a week of moderate intensity in pregnancy and after birth; some is better than none |
| Australia – Department of Health | Be active on most days, 30–60 minutes of moderate intensity; can be split up |
| UK | Not verified |

Decision 103 cautions: avoid exercise in very hot, humid weather; drink enough; avoid activities with a high risk of falls; **avoid long periods lying on your back after the first trimester**; stop if dizzy, short of breath or with chest pain. After a caesarean, ask a doctor before returning to exercise. [Verified]

**Foods and drinks to avoid or limit in pregnancy** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| UK – NHS (reviewed 15 Jun 2026) | Avoid: alcohol; unpasteurised dairy; raw or undercooked meat; liver and liver products (high in vitamin A); raw fish and shellfish; swordfish, shark, marlin. Limit: caffeine to 200 mg a day; oily fish to 2 portions a week |
| Alcohol – Australia, UK, US | All three advise not drinking in pregnancy. Australia advises not drinking while breastfeeding. See [Alcohol](/en/eating/alcohol/) |
| Vietnam | No official list verified |

NHS: 200 mg of caffeine is about 2 cups of instant coffee. See also [Caffeine](/en/eating/caffeine/). [Verified]

**Micronutrients and antenatal care** [Verified]

| Standard | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| WHO – Antenatal care (2016) | Daily iron and folic acid supplements in pregnancy to prevent anaemia and low birth weight. At least 8 contacts with a health worker during pregnancy |
| WHO – eLENA (updated 26 Jul 2024) | Folic acid should start as early as possible, ideally before conception |
| Vietnam | No document verified on number of antenatal visits or supplements |

Dose and type of supplement: set by a health worker after a check-up. The site gives no doses. [Expert judgement]

## How to do it – the minimal version

1. **See a health worker early** once you know you are pregnant, and keep to the check-up schedule. [Verified – WHO]
2. **Eat by your stage's pyramid column.** No need to "eat for two" from the start: grains are 12 units in the first trimester, 13.5 in the third. [Verified]
3. **Cook food thoroughly.** Avoid raw or rare meat, raw eggs, raw seafood, unpasteurised milk. [Verified – NHS]
4. **No alcohol, no tobacco.** Caffeine under 200 mg a day. [Verified]
5. **Brisk walking, swimming, pregnancy yoga** adding up to 150 minutes a week, unless a doctor has told you to limit activity. Pelvic floor exercises daily. [Verified – Decision 103]
6. **While breastfeeding:** use the "Breastfeeding" column; the pyramid gives about 11 cups of 200 ml water a day. [Verified]

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Pregnant women should rest, not move."** Decision 103 and WHO advise staying active through pregnancy if there is no contraindication. [Verified]
- **"You must eat double."** The pyramid rises step by step, not double. Protein, for example, goes from 5 units (first trimester) to 8 (third). [Verified]
- **"A little alcohol is fine."** UK: there is no safe level in pregnancy. [Verified]

## Who should take care

- If a doctor has told you to limit activity (for example bleeding, risk of preterm birth, low placenta): follow the doctor, not the general figures. [Expert judgement]
- Heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, anaemia, twins or more, severe morning sickness: food and activity need individual advice from a health worker. [Expert judgement]
- Vegans: need advice from a dietitian. See [Plant-based eating](/en/methods/plant-based-eating/). [Verified]
- The site gives no weight-gain targets. The right amount differs between people; ask a health worker. [Expert judgement]

## When to see a health worker

Go to a health facility **straight away** or call **113** if you have: [Expert judgement]

- Vaginal bleeding, leaking fluid, severe abdominal pain.
- The baby moving less, or not moving.
- Severe headache, blurred vision, sudden swelling of the face and hands.
- Fever, vomiting so much you cannot eat or drink, shortness of breath, chest pain.
- After birth: low mood or anxiety lasting weeks, or thoughts of harming yourself or the baby. See [Find help](/en/find-help/).
