*The site is written for Vietnamese readers; the Vietnamese version is the original. Quotes from Vietnamese documents are unofficial translations.*

## Key points

- WHO (2023): **no level of alcohol consumption is safe** for health. IARC classifies alcohol as a **Group 1 carcinogen**, and it causes at least 7 types of cancer. [Verified]
- The less you drink, the lower the risk. Australia advises at most 10 standard drinks a week and 4 a day; the UK advises no more than 14 units a week. The two countries measure a "drink" differently. [Verified]
- In Vietnam, the law **bans driving with any alcohol in the blood or breath** and **bans people under 18 from drinking**. [Verified]

## What the standards say

| Standard | Amount | Who should not drink |
|---|---|---|
| WHO (statement in Lancet Public Health, 2023) | No safe level; cancer risk rises with the amount drunk | – |
| Vietnam – Law on Prevention and Control of Harmful Effects of Alcohol (44/2019/QH14, in force 1 Jan 2020) | No "safe" amount set. Inciting or forcing others to drink is prohibited (Art. 5(1)) | People under 18 (Art. 5(2)); people driving with alcohol in their blood or breath (Art. 5(6)) |
| Vietnam – Law on Road Traffic Order and Safety (36/2024/QH15, consolidated 23 Mar 2026) | – | Driving a road vehicle with alcohol in the blood or breath is prohibited (Art. 9(2)) |
| US – DGA 2025–2030 (p. 4) | "Consume less alcohol for better overall health"; **no numeric limits any more** | Pregnant women; people recovering from alcohol use disorder or unable to control their drinking; people taking medicines or with conditions that interact with alcohol |
| Australia – NHMRC (2020) | Healthy adults: no more than **10 standard drinks a week** and **4 on any one day**; 1 standard drink = 10 g of pure alcohol | Under 18; pregnant or breastfeeding women |
| UK – CMOs (2016) | No more than **14 units a week** regularly, spread over 3 or more days; 1 unit = 8 g of alcohol | Pregnancy: no safe level |

The new US guidance is contested: the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases (AASLD) objected to removing daily limits. [Verified]

## How much alcohol is in a drink

Formula: **grams of alcohol = volume (ml) × strength (%) ÷ 100 × 0.789.** 0.789 is the density of alcohol (g/ml). [Inference]

| Drink | Estimated alcohol |
|---|---|
| 1 can of beer, 330 ml, 5% | about 13 g |
| 1 glass of wine, 150 ml, 13% | about 15 g |
| 1 shot of spirits, 30 ml, 40% | about 9.5 g |

[Hypothetical example] – read the strength on the label to calculate it properly. So one can of beer is already more than one Australian standard drink (10 g). [Inference]

## How to do it – the minimal version

**Step 1. If you don't drink, don't start for health reasons.** WHO: no studies show that possible benefits for heart disease or diabetes outweigh the cancer risk at the same level of drinking. [Verified]

**Step 2. If you drink, count the grams of alcohol in your week.** Use the formula above. [Inference]

**Step 3. Have several alcohol-free days a week.** The UK suggests this as a good way to cut down. Do not "save up" the week for one or two drinking sessions. [Verified]

**Step 4. Drink slowly, with food, and alternate with water.** The UK health department suggested this when it launched the 2016 guidelines. [Verified]

**Step 5. If you have been drinking, don't drive.** Vietnamese law bans any alcohol while driving. Take a taxi or a motorbike taxi, or ride with someone who has not been drinking. [Verified for the law; the rest is Inference]

**Step 6. Don't push drinks on others.** The law prohibits inciting or forcing others to drink. Saying "no" or holding a glass of water is normal. [Verified for the law]

**Step 7. Avoid alcohol in the 4 hours before bed.** See [Habits that help sleep](/en/sleep/sleep-habits/). [Verified]

## Common misunderstandings

- **"Red wine is good for the heart."** WHO: no studies show that heart benefits outweigh the cancer risk. The UK notes a possible protective effect only in women over 55 drinking very little. [Verified]
- **"Beer is weaker than spirits, so it's fine."** The harm comes from the alcohol, not the type of drink. 10 g of alcohol in beer, wine or spirits is the same. [Verified]
- **"Drinking a little has nothing to do with cancer."** In the WHO European Region, about half of alcohol-attributable cancers occur in people whose drinking is not classed as heavy. [Verified]
- **"After a night's sleep I can drive."** Alcohol leaves the body gradually; there may still be alcohol on the breath the next morning. The law allows no alcohol at all. [Expert judgement for the elimination part; Verified for the law]

## Who should take care

- **People under 18:** the law bans drinking. [Verified]
- **Pregnant women:** Australia, the UK and the US all advise not drinking. **Breastfeeding:** Australia advises not drinking. [Verified]
- **People taking medicines or with conditions that may interact with alcohol:** the DGA advises not drinking; ask a health worker. [Verified]
- **People who find it hard to control their drinking:** the DGA advises not drinking. **With a family history of alcoholism:** the DGA advises being mindful of how much you drink. [Verified]
- **Home-made or unlabelled spirits:** may contain methanol and cause serious poisoning. [Expert judgement]

## When to see a health worker

- You want to cut down or stop but cannot. [Expert judgement]
- Shaking hands, sweating, restlessness or poor sleep when you stop: stopping suddenly after heavy drinking can be dangerous and needs medical care. [Expert judgement]
- Confusion, heavy vomiting, trouble breathing, blurred vision, seizures, or not waking up after drinking: **call emergency services now** (see [Find help](/en/find-help/)). [Expert judgement]

## Level of evidence

- **Alcohol causes cancer: High.** IARC places alcohol in Group 1, the category with the strongest evidence. [Verified]
- **Risk at low intake: Moderate.** The numeric limits (Australia, UK) are based on reviews and lifetime-risk modelling. Australia estimates that drinking no more than 10 standard drinks a week and 4 a day keeps the lifetime risk of dying from alcohol-related disease or injury below 1 in 100. [Verified]
