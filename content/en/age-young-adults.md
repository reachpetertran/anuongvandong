## Key points

- Most standards have **no separate 18–39 group**. People this age follow the general adult recommendations (usually 18–64). (drawn from the table below) [Inference]
- So the articles below are adult articles. This page collects them and adds a few points specific to ages 18–39.
- Specific points: bone mass can keep growing until about age 30; young adults may need more than 9 hours of sleep; caffeine and alcohol are common at study and work ages.

## Minimum daily dose

1. **Eating:** don't add extra fish sauce or seasoning powder; swap sugary drinks for water or unsweetened tea. See [Salt](/en/eating/salt/), [Free sugars](/en/eating/free-sugars/).
2. **Movement:** about 30 minutes of brisk walking on most days, plus 2 strength sessions a week. See [Active minutes](/en/movement/active-minutes/), [Strength and balance](/en/movement/strength-balance/).
3. **Sitting:** if you study or work seated for long periods, get up and move often. See [Sitting less](/en/movement/sitting-less/).
4. **Sleep:** 7 hours or more, with a fixed wake-up time. See [How much sleep](/en/sleep/how-much-sleep/), [Sleep habits](/en/sleep/sleep-habits/).
5. **Breathing, meditation:** 5 minutes of slow breathing or 10 minutes of mindfulness, if it suits you. See [Minimal sleep, breathing and meditation](/en/methods/minimal-sleep-breathing-meditation/).

## What is specific to ages 18–39

**Bones are still "saving up".** According to the NIH (US), bone mass can keep growing until **about age 30**, when it reaches its peak (peak bone mass). Young adults who exercise regularly generally reach a higher peak. (archived copy: NIH page, January 2012) [Verified] The NHS advises weight-bearing, strengthening and balance exercise; not smoking; and not drinking too much alcohol. [Verified] See [Strength and balance](/en/movement/strength-balance/).

**Sleep.** The AASM and SRS advise adults to sleep 7 hours or more. More than 9 hours may suit young adults or people catching up on lost sleep. [Verified] Late-night study, extra jobs and phone use often shift sleep times. [Inference]

**Coffee, milk tea, energy drinks.** These are common sources of caffeine and sugar at study and work ages. [Inference] See the new article [Caffeine: coffee, tea, energy drinks](/en/eating/caffeine/).

**Alcohol.** WHO: there is no safe level of drinking. Vietnamese law bans driving with any alcohol in the blood or breath. [Verified] See [Alcohol](/en/eating/alcohol/).

**Planning a baby.** WHO advises starting folic acid as early as possible, ideally before conception. See [Pregnancy and breastfeeding](/en/age/pregnancy-breastfeeding/). If you are planning a pregnancy, talk to a health worker first. [Verified for the WHO advice]

## Who should take care

- People with a long-term condition, taking medicines, pregnant or breastfeeding: the articles are written for healthy adults and may not apply to you. [Expert judgement]
- People with eating or weight concerns: read with the support of a health worker. [Expert judgement]

## When to see a health worker

- Lasting tiredness, weeks of poor sleep, weight loss without a clear reason. [Expert judgement]
- Chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting: **call emergency services** (see [Find help](/en/find-help/)).
